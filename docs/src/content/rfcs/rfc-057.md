## Context

[RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048) lets a policy bind an open term of the law to the answer of a cell. An article of the policy fills the term (`implements`) and takes the answer as an input (`source.reduction`). The engine asks the cell that the term names in its `competent_authority`, or else the cell of the policy's holder. The answer then flows into the decision of the cell that runs the execution.

Nothing checked whose policy the binding sat in. A municipality's decision would take the answer of the Chamber of Commerce through a binding in any policy that filled the term, including the Chamber's own policy or that of a third body. In law the receiving authority needs a legal basis (*grondslag*) to use what another organization holds. An authority cannot give itself that right in the middle of a decision.

The same holds for a form that is prefilled before the applicant submits it. The proof of concept prefills an application from an output that a cell consults (`Cell::consult`). That output could be any article of any rulework, including a policy of the source or a reduction.

## Decision

### 1. The receiver needs a ground for an answer of another cell

The receiver is the cell that runs the execution (`Call::answerer`). When a binding is about to ask another cell, the receiver must have one of these grounds:

- **Its own policy.** The binding is in a policy whose holder (`competent_authority` at the root) names the receiver, by name or by category. A cell that decides in place of another authority may use that authority's policy (§2).
- **A law that gives the term to the holder.** A term the binding fills names the holder of the policy in its own `competent_authority`. The law then makes the holder competent for the term, and the holder's policy says how it fills it. The receiver applies the law when it uses the answer. A holder is one authority by name. Where the term names a category, the holder belongs to it when the cell in the call with the holder's name does.

Without a ground the engine refuses the input, before it asks any cell. The error names the policy, its holder and the receiver, and refers to this RFC.

An answer from the receiver's own chronicle crosses no cell and needs no ground. When no cell is asked, nothing flows and nothing is checked. The binding then gives what it gives without an answer ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048) §2).

The first ground is the usual form. The decider's policy has an article that fills the term of the law and takes the other cell's answer. The second covers a law that delegates the term to the source itself. In that case the source's policy fills the term, and the law is the ground for whoever applies it.

### 2. A court that decides the case itself decides for the body

A court that decides the case itself (Awb 8:72 lid 3 onder b) takes the decision of the administrative body again, with that body's policy. The Awb gives the court that power, and with it the body's ground. A `Call` therefore names the authority its cell decides for (`Call::decides_for`), and the first ground also holds for that authority's policy.

The cell in `packages/cel` sets `decides_for` when it decides on a submission against a decision of another cell, as an appeal is ([RFC-053](/rfcs/rfc-053)). It names the authority of the cell that took the contested decision, found among the cells the decision takes along. A replay of such a decision sets it the same way.

### 3. The engine checks, when it resolves the binding

The check sits in the engine, at the moment it resolves an input with `source.reduction`. Only then is the receiver known. The loader sees the bindings, but not which cell will run them. The cell sees the receiver, but learns of a binding only after the engine has asked the source.

### 4. A form prefills only from a law or the cell's own policy

`Cell::consult` refuses a source in a `REDUCTIE`, and a source in a `UITVOERINGSBELEID` whose holder does not name the consulting cell. A law or regulation of any other layer is a ground, and so is the cell's own policy. A binding reached from either is checked by the engine as in §1.

A prefill therefore points at an output of the cell's own policy or of the law, such as the article that binds the term of the law to the register's answer. It does not point at the register's policy or reduction.

## Why

### Benefits

- The rulework shows the ground. A reader finds it in the policy of the deciding authority, or in the law that names the source as competent.
- One rule covers the decision, the replay, the decision of a court in place of the body, and the prefilled form. The engine still only compares names, as in [RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048).

### Tradeoffs

- A policy without scope serves only its holder when another cell's answer is needed. Several bodies that decide under the same law need a policy each. Before, one fictional policy could serve every college of a test.
- Whether a holder belongs to a category is only known from a cell in the call. A delegation by category therefore holds only when the holder's cell is part of the call.
- `decides_for` follows from the copy a submission received. It holds for every decision on an appeal, not only for one that the court takes in place of the body. A judgment that does not take the decision again reaches no binding of the body's policy, so this changes nothing there.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Only the receiver's own policy.** This leaves out a law that gives the term to the source. That form is correct in law: the law is then the ground.
- **A check in the loader.** The loader does not know which cell receives, so it can check only who holds the policy, not who uses it.
- **A check in the cell after the execution.** The source has then already computed its answer, and the check is spread over every way a cell calls the engine.
- **A court is always grounded.** That is an exception by name, not a ground that the rulework shows.

## Implementation

The engine has `Call::decides_for`, the check in `resolve_answer` (`ungrounded`, `names_holder` in `service.rs`) and tests in `packages/engine/tests/synthesis.rs`. The cell sets `decides_for` in `Cell::decide` and in the replay, and checks the source of `Cell::consult` (`refuse_source_without_ground`, tests in `packages/cel/tests/consult.rs`). The court in the proof of concept relies on `decides_for` when it decides the case itself (`packages/cel/tests/beroep.rs`, `packages/poc-chronolexografie/src/kern.rs`).

No schema version changes. The bindings in the corpus of the proof of concept were already in the policy of the authority that decides. Test fixtures in which another cell decided under that policy now give the policy to the deciding cell.
