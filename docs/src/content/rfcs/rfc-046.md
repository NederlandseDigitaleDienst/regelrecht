## Context

[RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022) takes its vocabulary from the [Chronolexografie position paper](https://chronolexografie.nl/position-paper/). In that vocabulary a cell is an organization that keeps its own chronicle and answers questions from it. The chronicle is a list of dated recordings, each of which is a chronolexogram. A submission, a decision and a recorded fact are all chronolexograms.

An earlier proof of concept put a block of recording rules in each article that establishes a submission or a decision. Most of that block repeated what the law already said. This RFC decides where the recording code lives, and where a recording gets its shape from.

## Decision

[Chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography) shows this at work.

1. **Recording is an optional layer.** It lives in its own crate, `packages/cel`. That crate depends on the law and on the engine. Neither of them depends on it, so another runtime may record the same law in a different way.
2. **A cell is a directory that holds `cel.yaml`.** The file says who the cell is, and which answers it gives through which reduction (`answers`, [RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048) §1). A reduction is a rulework that reads the chronicle and gives named answers. The file is only a catalogue. It holds no logic. The reductions themselves sit beside it, under `reductions/`.

   The law names a role, but it cannot say which organization a given cell is. So `cel.yaml` is the only configuration a cell has. The engine compares the identity in it with the `competent_authority` of the law ([RFC-051](/rfcs/rfc-051)). A cell without `cel.yaml` does not open.
3. **The law gives each recording its shape.** The cell refuses any field that is not in that shape, and any value of the wrong type.

   - A submission holds the parameters of its `produces` item and of the hooks that apply to it, minus the `moment` ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)). It holds no outputs. What its article and those hooks give, such as the last day of the decision period (Awb 4:13), follows from its fields and the law in force on `recorded_on`, so the cell computes it when asked.
   - A decision holds its inputs, including the findings, and its outputs. A finding is what the authority itself decides on an open norm in a single case.
   - An executogram records a fact that an article `requires`, such as a completed medical examination. It holds the parameters of that fact ([RFC-051](/rfcs/rfc-051)).
4. **A chronolexogram does not repeat the law.** Its keys follow RFC-022 §1.3 (`recording_actor`, `fields`, and `basis` for the article it is recorded under). It stores nothing the law already says: that comes from the version of the law in force on `recorded_on` ([Chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography#what-a-chronicle-holds)). Numbers are stored without loss of precision (`regelrecht_law_model::stored`).
5. **The cell guards the chronicle.** The chronicle only grows, and its entries are in date order. One submission gets one decision. A decision can be replaced at most once; the Awb has no such rule, and this RFC adds it. [Chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography#recording-and-deciding) lists every refusal.
6. **A decision is explained by taking it again.** The cell does not store an explanation. It retakes the decision on the day it was taken, with the law and the chronicle as they were then ([Chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography#explaining-a-decision)). This assumes that every source can say what it knew on a given day.

```json
{
  "id": "019a1b2c-…",
  "kind": "AANVRAAG",
  "basis": "leerplichtwet_1969#13a",
  "recording_actor": "obs_de_klimop",
  "recorded_on": "2026-10-13",
  "moment": { "parameter": "datum_ontvangst", "on": "2026-10-13", "declared_by": "algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:13" },
  "fields": { "eerste_verlofdag": "2027-02-01", "adres_aanvrager": null }
}
```

## Why

### Benefits

- A rulework holds no recording vocabulary. A jurist can read it without knowing what a cell is.
- When an Awb article starts asking for one more field, every recording follows through one new version of the Awb rulework.
- One `record_submission` and one `decide` serve every procedure. A second case needs rulework and tests, but no new code.

### Tradeoffs

- The cell does not store which provision each field came from. To learn what a decision used, the cell has to take it again.
- The cell computes what follows from a submission again for every question. After a correction of the model, the answer is the corrected one, not the one given at receipt.

Open questions are listed in [Open Questions in Chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography/open-questions).

### Alternatives Considered

- **A chronolexography block in the law** (RFC-022 §3.2 `extensions`). This puts recording vocabulary in a rulework, and most of the block repeats the law.
- **A list of fields in the cell configuration, or a stream of recordings in `chronicles/`** (RFC-022 §1.3). Both drift away from the law when it changes.
- **The legal basis per field** (`field_basis`, `output_basis`). This repeats the law in every entry.
- **The cell runs the objection procedure** (`record_objection`, `decide_on_objection`). That put Awb 7:11 in Rust code. The objection is now rulework ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)).
- **A decision taken ex officio** (`Cell::decide_ex_officio`), for the holidays a school sets itself. Dropped on 19 October 2026, because the holidays are policy, not a decision.
- **The place of establishment as part of the cell's identity.** A place can change over time, so it is recorded as an executogram instead (decided 18 October 2026). The paper defines an executogram as "de vastlegging van daadwerkelijke levering of afhandeling", which fits a place of establishment only loosely. The paper does call its three types a first enumeration, so the list is not closed.

## Implementation

In `packages/cel`, `shape` derives the fields from the law. The cell offers `Cell::record_submission`, `submission_outputs`, `decide`, `record_executogram`, `explain` and `findings`. `Chronicle` holds the `Gram`s, and `identity::read` reads `cel.yaml`. The cells are under `corpus/cells/`, the tests in `packages/cel/tests/`.
