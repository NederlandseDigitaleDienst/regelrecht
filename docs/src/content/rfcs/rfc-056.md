## Context

Most values a law reads about a person or a company come from a register. The law format writes such a value as an input with `source: {}`: the value is resolved outside the rulework. The demo resolves it from `corpus/demo/bindings.yaml`, which names a table and a column per input. A scenario resolves it from a data table:

```gherkin
And the following "DOUANE" data with key "kvk_nummer" for law "wet_op_de_accijns/accijnsplicht_alcohol":
  | kvk_nummer | heeft_agp_vergunning_register |
  | 85234567   | false                         |
```

That table says what the register holds, but not who recorded it, when, or on what basis. A cell can say all of that ([RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046)). Yet a cell can only answer an open term today. [RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048) lets the policy of the deciding authority bind a term to a named answer of a cell, and that route assumes discretion: the decider chooses where an answer comes from.

A register is a different case. The Handelsregisterwet 2007 says that the Kamer van Koophandel keeps the handelsregister (art. 3 lid 1) and that the register holds "een door de Kamer toegekend uniek nummer" for each company (art. 9 onder a). Wet op de accijns art. 39 lets a place serve as an accijnsgoederenplaats only "indien daartoe een vergunning is verstrekt door de inspecteur". In both, the law itself names who establishes the fact. The authority that later reads the fact has no say in where it comes from.

The chronolexografie PoC already models the Kamer as a cell (`packages/poc-chronolexografie/corpus/cells/kamer_van_koophandel/`). To reach its answers, it needs the route of RFC-048: the fictional policy of a municipality binds open terms of a fictional parking regulation to the answers `postcode_vestiging` and `aantal_werkzame_personen`. The municipality has no discretion there, so the policy says nothing a jurist would recognize as policy. It exists because the format has no other path from a law to a keeper's answer.

This RFC adds that path.

## Decision

### 1. A register is a cell

A register entry is an act of the authority that a law designates to keep the register. The municipality records a person in the BRP (Wet BRP art. 1.4 lid 1), the Kamer records a company in the handelsregister, and the inspecteur grants a permit. Each such act is a chronolexogram in the chronicle of that authority's cell. What the register says on a given day is a reduction over that chronicle, run in the cell that keeps it.

Nothing about a cell changes. A keeper is a cell like any other, with a `cel.yaml`, a chronicle and reductions. What is new is how the law reaches it.

### 2. Two routes to a cell's answer

| | Open term (RFC-048, unchanged) | Fact a law establishes (this RFC) |
|---|---|---|
| The law says | an open term, left to the authority | a fact, with the authority that establishes it (`requires`, `competent_authority`, RFC-051) |
| Which cell answers | the cell the term names in `competent_authority`, or else the holder of the binding policy | the cell that the fact names in `competent_authority` |
| Which answer in that cell | the answer the decider's policy names (`source.reduction`) | the reduction that declares it implements the fact |
| Who maps question to answer | the deciding authority, in its `UITVOERINGSBELEID` | the keeper, in its reduction |

The second route has no policy of the decider in between, because the decider has no discretion over where register data comes from. The mapping from the fact to a reduction is still a choice, and arguably a policy choice. It is the keeper's choice, though, and it lives with the keeper's reduction.

### 3. The law names the fact and its keeper

The article that establishes a fact declares it under `requires`, with the authority that establishes it, as RFC-051 already allows. The same article reads the value with a new source key, `source.fact`, which names a fact of its own `requires`:

```yaml
# wet_op_de_accijns/accijnsplicht_alcohol, art. 39, proposed
machine_readable:
  requires:
    - fact: vergunning_accijnsgoederenplaats_verstrekt
      parameters: [kvk_nummer, datum_verstrekking, datum_intrekking]
      competent_authority:
        - name: de inspecteur
  execution:
    parameters:
      - name: kvk_nummer
        type: string
    input:
      - name: heeft_agp_vergunning_register
        type: boolean
        source:
          fact: vergunning_accijnsgoederenplaats_verstrekt
          parameters: {kvk_nummer: $kvk_nummer}
    output:
      - name: heeft_agp_vergunning
        type: boolean
```

Other articles and other laws keep reading the value as an output of this article, through `source.regulation` ([RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007)). Art. 5 of the same rulework already does so for `heeft_agp_vergunning`. The law names a role and never a cell, as RFC-051 §4 requires.

### 4. The keeper's reduction implements the fact

A reduction says which fact of which law it answers, with `implements`, in the shape that `open_terms` and `implements` already have between a law and its policy ([inversion of control](/concepts/inversion-of-control)):

```yaml
# corpus/cells/<inspecteur>/reductions/reductie_agp_vergunningen, shortened
regulatory_layer: REDUCTIE
implements:
  - law: wet_op_de_accijns
    article: '39'
    fact: vergunning_accijnsgoederenplaats_verstrekt
articles:
  - number: '1'
    machine_readable:
      execution:
        parameters: [kvk_nummer, peildatum]
        input:
          - name: vergunningen
            type: array
            source:
              facts: wet_op_de_accijns#39#vergunning_accijnsgoederenplaats_verstrekt
        output:
          - name: heeft_agp_vergunning_register
            type: boolean
```

The reduction answers with the output that carries the name of the reading input (`heeft_agp_vergunning_register`). A fact can be read in more than one way, for example whether a permit exists and since when, so one reduction may declare several outputs, and each input that reads the fact picks one by its name.

How the keeper recorded the act is its own business. Here the inspecteur records an executogram of the fact that art. 39 requires. A keeper that records the permit as a decision under another article reads `source.decisions` instead, and the law does not change.

### 5. The key travels as a parameter

The `with key "kvk_nummer"` of a data table becomes a parameter of the question: the input passes `parameters: {kvk_nummer: $kvk_nummer}`, and the reduction declares `kvk_nummer` as a parameter. The engine hands over the calculation date as `peildatum`.

A key is often itself a fact a keeper establishes. The law that makes the Kamer keep the register also makes it the issuer of the number (Handelsregisterwet 2007 art. 9 onder a, for a company, and art. 11 lid 1 onder a, for an establishment). A citizen's BSN is issued under the Wet algemene bepalingen burgerservicenummer. The key is not a parameter the case invents.

The key does not have to come from a register either. A caller may pass a KVK number by hand, and the law does not ask where it came from.

### 6. The cell is one way to supply a value, never the only one

A law stays executable on its own. Every input that reads a fact can still be supplied by hand: as a parameter, from a data table or a data source of the call, or by a simulation. `competent_authority` on a fact says who is competent by law to establish it. It is information about the law, and the engine does not read it as a rule that the value must come from that cell.

So the engine resolves an input with `source.fact` as follows:

| Supplied by the caller | Competent cell in the call | Result |
|---|---|---|
| yes | no | the supplied value |
| no | yes | the answer of the cell |
| yes | yes | an error: the engine does not choose between two sources for one value |
| no | no | the input has no value, as a `source: {}` input without data has none today ([RFC-036](/rfcs/rfc-036) decides what that means) |

The third row follows RFC-048 §2, where a finding on a term that a policy fills makes the execution fail rather than letting one silently win. A caller that wants a cell to answer leaves the value out, and a caller that supplies the value leaves the cell out. Two cells that the fact names in one call are an error too, as in RFC-048 §4. Whether a supplied value should instead take precedence over the cell, as a correction by hand, is an open question below.

Recording stays restricted and consuming does not. A cell records a fact only when the fact names that organization (RFC-051 §2), and that rule is unchanged. Who supplies the value to a consuming law is not restricted, because the consumer has no way to verify it anyway and the law does not require it.

The trace and the execution result say where each such value came from. An input resolved from the caller's data is marked as supplied, with the source it came from. An input a cell answered is marked as answered, with the cell, the reduction and its version, and how far the reduction read the chronicle, as for an open term (RFC-048 §5). This sits next to the provenance of outputs (direct, reactive or override, which the Gherkin steps already assert), and it is about inputs.

### 7. Hand-supplied and chronicle scenarios

Both kinds of scenario are first-class. Neither replaces the other.

- **Hand-supplied scenarios** test a rulework on its own. They give facts with data tables and parameters, and open terms with `findings`. That is how most of the corpus is tested, and it stays so: the law can be read and checked without any cell.
- **Chronicle scenarios** test the chain end to end. They record grams in the cells, and evaluate with those cells in the call, so the keepers' real reductions answer. A chronicle scenario needs no mock reductions. Where a test wants to stand in for a cell, it supplies the value by hand, which is what a data table or a finding already does.

A scenario may mix the two: one fact answered by a cell, another supplied in a table. Only the same input from both sources is an error (§6).

### What this changes in RFC-048 and RFC-051

- RFC-048 §1 says a reduction fills no open term and has no `implements`. A reduction still fills no open term. It may now carry `implements` entries with a `fact` and no `open_term`.
- RFC-048 §2, where the decider's policy binds a term to an answer, stays as it is for open terms. For a fact, the law decides who answers, and no binding sits in between.
- RFC-048 §3 lets only a reduction read a chronicle. That stays: `source.fact` asks a cell a question, and only the cell's reduction reads the chronicle.
- RFC-051 §2 says a term that reads a fact must repeat the fact's competent authorities, because the engine does not link the term to the fact. With `source.fact`, the engine does link the input to the fact, so for this route the open question in RFC-051 is answered: nothing is repeated. An open term that reads a fact keeps the old rule.
- RFC-051 §4 says the law reads an established value through `source.regulation` from the establishing article. That stays. `source.fact` appears only in the establishing article itself.

## Worked example: the AGP permit and the handelsregister

The scenario in the context checks whether a brewery with KVK number 85234567 may produce without a permit for an accijnsgoederenplaats. Today the data table states that the Douane register holds no permit.

As a chronicle scenario the same case reads:

```gherkin
Background:
  Given the calculation date is "2024-06-01"
  And parameter "kvk_nummer" is "85234567"
  And the cell "inspecteur_fictief" is in the call

Scenario: Produceren met een ingetrokken vergunning
  Given the cell "inspecteur_fictief" records fact "wet_op_de_accijns#39#vergunning_accijnsgoederenplaats_verstrekt" on "2022-02-01":
    | kvk_nummer         | 85234567   |
    | datum_verstrekking | 2022-02-01 |
    | datum_intrekking   |            |
  And the cell "inspecteur_fictief" records fact "wet_op_de_accijns#39#vergunning_accijnsgoederenplaats_verstrekt" on "2024-03-15":
    | kvk_nummer         | 85234567   |
    | datum_verstrekking | 2022-02-01 |
    | datum_intrekking   | 2024-03-15 |
  And parameter "activiteit" is "productie"
  When I evaluate outputs "heeft_agp_vergunning, voldoet_aan_vergunningsplicht" of "wet_op_de_accijns/accijnsplicht_alcohol"
  Then output "heeft_agp_vergunning" is false
  And output "voldoet_aan_vergunningsplicht" is false
  And input "heeft_agp_vergunning_register" was answered by cell "inspecteur_fictief"
```

The engine reaches art. 39, finds `source.fact`, and takes the fact's `competent_authority`. The cell `inspecteur_fictief` matches it. The cell finds the reduction that implements the fact, runs it on its chronicle with `kvk_nummer` and the calculation date, and answers false: the permit was revoked before 1 June 2024. The trace records who answered, from which reduction, and how far it read the chronicle, as for an open term (RFC-048 §5).

The permit was never in the Douane "register" as a row. It is in the inspecteur's chronicle as two dated acts, and the scenario can show a case that a single row cannot: a permit that held in 2023 and no longer holds.

The scenario from the context keeps working as it is. Its data table supplies `heeft_agp_vergunning_register` for KVK number 85234567, no cell is in the call, and the engine takes the supplied value (the first row of the table in §6). The two scenarios test different things: the hand-supplied one tests the accijns rules on their own, the chronicle one also tests the inspecteur's reduction.

The Kamer van Koophandel fits the same pattern. Handelsregisterwet 2007 art. 11 lid 1 lists what the register holds about an establishment, among which "het post- en bezoekadres" (onder c). The PoC's reduction `reductie_vestiging_in_handelsregister` would declare `implements` for a fact `vestiging_ingeschreven` on that article. The fictional policy `beleid_handelsregister_kamer_van_koophandel` now carries that fact because the Handelsregisterwet 2007 is not in the corpus. Once it is, the fact moves to the law, and the parking policy of Wilgendaal no longer needs a binding for the postcode.

## Where a `source: {}` value can come from

Every value a law reads can be supplied by hand (§6). The question here is a different one: for which values does a cell route exist at all? A value can reach a law through a cell in three ways:

- **(a)** as a fact kept by a keeper cell, which this RFC covers;
- **(b)** as a submission recorded in the deciding cell, such as a statement in an application ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045));
- **(c)** as an open term answered under RFC-048, or as the finding of the deciding authority.

Values that fit none of these have no cell route. The caller supplies them, as it does today, and that is not a defect.

We went through the 282 `source: {}` inputs in `corpus/demo/regulation` (200), `corpus/regulation` (63) and the chronolexografie PoC corpus (19), using the demo bindings to see where each value comes from.

| Route | Inputs | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| (a) register of a public authority | about 145 in the demo, most of the 62 elsewhere | BRP (RvIG, municipalities), Belastingdienst income components, KVK, UWV, SVB, DUO, DJI, Bibob advice (Landelijk Bureau Bibob) |
| (b) submission or case | 17 | huurprijs and childcare hours in an application, procedural events in Awb 7:1 and 8:1 |
| (c) open term or finding | about 21 | the Awb art. 1:1 classification of an organization (`is_rechterlijk_orgaan`, `overheidsfinanciering_percentage`), `is_ingezetene` in the Wlz, where the children live |

Some values have a cell route only with a qualification:

- **Keepers that are not administrative bodies (9 demo inputs, plus `polis_status` in the Zvw).** A pension fund, a health insurer, a notary and the Stichting Vakbekwaamheid Horeca keep records a law relies on. RFC-051 does not restrict `competent_authority` to administrative bodies, so the format allows such a keeper. Whether the project wants one is open.
- **Statistics (1 input).** The CBS life expectancy fits (a): AOW art. 7a lid 4 says the estimates "worden uitgevoerd en bekendgemaakt door het CBS". It is not a fact about a person, though. The demo and its scenarios look it up by BSN, while its real key is the year.

Other values have no cell route, and the caller supplies them:

- **The legal order itself (7 inputs).** The Awb objection and appeal articles read `wet`, `termijn_weken`, `beslistermijn_weken` and a `jurisdictie` from reference tables, and an APV reads a terrace tariff per square meter from one. These values are law or policy. In time they belong in a rulework read through `source.regulation`, which is a separate change.
- **The polling day.** The Kieswet reads `verkiezingsdatum` as `source: {}`. It is no fact about the voter but part of what is asked: whether someone may vote in this election.
- **Engine fixtures (20 inputs).** The `test_null_semantics`, `test_nullable` and `test_scoped_source_bron` ruleworks test how data sources behave, and stay as they are.

The calculation date is not among the inputs at all. It is part of the question, the day the caller asks about, and the engine hands it to a reduction as `peildatum`. The alcohol percentage and the quantity in the accijns case are parameters of the declaration, so (b).

Most register values have a cell route. Most of the rest are submissions or findings, and what remains is part of the question or of the law.

## Why

### Benefits

- A register value gets provenance: who recorded it, on which day, and on what legal basis. A data table has none of that.
- The law stays in its own terms. It says that the inspecteur grants the permit, which is what art. 39 says, and the format needs no word for register or cell.
- No fictional policy of the decider is needed where the decider has no choice. The PoC's binding for the KVK postcode disappears.
- Hand-supplied scenarios and the demo bindings keep working unchanged (§6), so moving an input to `source.fact` does not have to wait for its cells.
- A scenario can express time: a permit granted and revoked, an address that changed. A row holds one state.

### Tradeoffs

- The keeper's mapping from fact to reduction is a choice that nobody outside the cell reviews, unlike a published policy.
- An input only gets a cell route once its keeper has a cell with a reduction. For the demo that is roughly forty services, many with several tables. Until then the input is supplied by hand, as today.
- Because the engine accepts a supplied value (§6), it cannot tell a deliberate hand-supplied value from a cell someone forgot to put in the call. The provenance in the trace shows which happened, but nothing refuses it.
- A fact and an open term are two routes to a cell, and an author has to choose between them per input. The rule of thumb is discretion: if the deciding authority chooses where the value comes from, it is an open term.

### Alternatives Considered

**A policy binding for every fact, as in the PoC.**
- Every register value would become an open term with a `UITVOERINGSBELEID` of the decider binding it to an answer.
- Rejected: the decider has no discretion, so the policy is fiction. It also makes every consuming authority write the same binding again for the same register.

**The keeper's catalogue maps the fact.**
- `cel.yaml` would list per fact which reduction answers it, as it lists named answers today.
- Rejected for now: the catalogue holds no logic, and `implements` on the reduction keeps the mapping next to the code that answers and in the same shape as policy implementing a law. The catalogue can still be derived from it (see implementation).

**The law names the reduction or the cell.**
- `source.fact` would name a reduction or a cell directly.
- Rejected: RFC-051 §4. The law names a role, and which organization fills the role can differ per case and over time.

**Keep data sources.**
- `source: {}` stays and the cell writes a table that the data source reads.
- Rejected: the table loses the dated acts behind it, which is what the cell adds.

## Implementation sketch

None of this is built.

- **Schema (v0.8.14).** `source.fact` on an input, with `parameters` beside it and no other key. An `implements` entry with `law`, `article` and `fact`, allowed only on a `REDUCTIE`, and on a `REDUCTIE` only with `fact`. The rest of RFC-048 §3 stays.
- **Loader checks.** `source.fact` must name a fact of the article's own `requires` that has a `competent_authority`. An `implements` with a `fact` must name a fact that exists in that law and article. Whoever loads a corpus with cells checks that the cell keeping such a reduction is one the fact names.
- **Engine.** A new question kind next to `AnswerRequest`: the law, article and fact, the output asked for, the parameters, and the calculation date. `OpenTermAnswerer` gets a method for it, or a second trait does. Resolution follows the table in §6: look for a supplied value in the data sources of the call, and for answerers in the call that the fact's `competent_authority` names. The trace and `ArticleResult` record per input whether it was supplied or answered, and by whom.
- **Cell.** The cell indexes its reductions by `implements` when it opens, refuses two reductions that implement the same fact, and answers a fact question by running the reduction that implements it. `cel.yaml` keeps its `answers` for open terms. For facts it needs no entry, and `Cell::answers` can list the derived ones next to the declared ones.
- **Gherkin.** New steps in `bdd/grammar.yaml`, in a tier of their own: a cell is in the call, a cell records a fact on a day (a vertical table of the fact's parameters), and later a cell records a decision. Next to the output provenance steps comes an assertion on an input: that it was answered by a given cell, or supplied. The Rust runner in `packages/engine/tests/bdd` needs `regelrecht-cel` as a dev-dependency. Cargo accepts that cycle for integration tests, since `regelrecht-cel` depends on the engine. The JS runner in `packages/frontend-shared` drives the engine's WASM build, which has no cell. Until it does, it should skip such a scenario with a stated reason rather than fail. `docs/scripts/check-gherkin-steps.mjs` and the demo's Gherkin templates read the grammar too.
- **Where demo cells live.** `corpus/demo/cells/<cell>/`, next to `corpus/demo/regulation/`, like the PoC.
- **Docs.** The glossary entries for reduction and `REDUCTIE`, the [chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography) page and [testing](/guide/testing) describe the second route and the chronicle scenarios.

## Open questions

- **Registers with many keepers.** The BRP has one keeper per municipality for its residents, and the minister for non-residents (Wet BRP art. 1.4). RFC-048 §4 sends one question to one cell, and a call with two cells that the fact names is an error. For a person, only one municipality keeps the entry on a given day, but which one is itself a fact in the register. The minister is responsible for most provision of data (art. 1.5 lid 1), which suggests a single answering cell holding copies ([RFC-053](/rfcs/rfc-053)), but that makes the minister answer for what the municipalities record. This needs its own decision before the BRP can move.
- **A fact of another article.** `source.fact` names a fact of the article's own `requires`. A law that establishes a fact in one article and reads it in another uses `source.regulation` between them. Whether that is enough everywhere is untested.
- **Private keepers.** See the survey: may a keeper be a pension fund or an insurer?
- **The legal order in reference tables.** The Awb inputs that read a law, a period or a jurisdiction from a table should become rulework. That is a separate change, not part of this RFC.
- **A supplied value and a cell answer together.** §6 makes that an error, in line with RFC-048 §2. The alternative is that the supplied value wins and the trace marks it as a correction by hand. That would let a scenario overrule one answer of a cell it otherwise uses, but it is a priority rule the engine adds, which RFC-048 avoids.
- **A caller that wants the cell.** The engine never forces the cell route. A production caller that must not run on hand-supplied register data has to check the provenance itself, or ask for a mode in which a fact without a cell answer is an error. Whether such a mode belongs in the engine is open.
