## Context

Most values a law reads about a person or a company come from a register. The law format writes such a value as an input with `source: {}`: the value is resolved outside the rulework. The demo resolves it from `corpus/demo/bindings.yaml`, which names a table and a column per input. A scenario resolves it from a data table:

```gherkin
And the following "DOUANE" data with key "kvk_nummer" for law "wet_op_de_accijns/accijnsplicht_alcohol":
  | kvk_nummer | heeft_agp_vergunning_register |
  | 85234567   | false                         |
```

That table says what the register holds, but not who recorded it, when, or on what basis. A cell can say all of that ([RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046)). Yet a cell can only answer an open term today. [RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048) lets the policy of the deciding authority bind a term to a named answer of a cell, and that route assumes discretion: the decider chooses where an answer comes from.

A register is a different case. The Handelsregisterwet 2007 says that the Kamer van Koophandel keeps the handelsregister (art. 3 lid 1) and that the register holds "een door de Kamer toegekend uniek nummer" for each company (art. 9 onder a). Wet op de accijns art. 39 lets a place serve as an accijnsgoederenplaats only "indien daartoe een vergunning is verstrekt door de inspecteur". In both, the law itself names who establishes the fact. The authority that later reads the fact has no say in where it comes from.

The chronolexografie PoC already models the Kamer as a cell (`packages/poc-chronolexografie/corpus/cells/kamer_van_koophandel/`). To reach its answers, it needs the route of RFC-048: the fictional policy of a municipality binds open terms of a fictional parking regulation to the answers `postcode_vestiging` and `aantal_werkzame_personen`. The municipality has no discretion there, so the policy says nothing a jurist would recognize as policy. It exists because the format has no other path from a law to a keeper's answer.

This RFC adds that path.

## Decision

### 1. A register is a cell

A register entry is an act of the authority that a law designates to keep the register. The municipality records a person in the BRP (Wet BRP art. 1.4 lid 1), the Kamer records a company in the handelsregister, and the inspecteur grants a permit. Each such act is a chronolexogram in the chronicle of that authority's cell. What the register says on a given day is a reduction over that chronicle, run in the cell that keeps it.

Nothing about a cell changes. A keeper is a cell like any other, with a `cel.yaml`, a chronicle and reductions. What is new is how the law reaches it.

### 2. Two routes to a cell's answer

| | Open term (RFC-048, unchanged) | Input a competent authority answers (this RFC) |
|---|---|---|
| The law says | an open term, left to the authority | an input whose source names the competent authority (`source.competent_authority`) |
| Which cell answers | the cell the term names in `competent_authority`, or else the holder of the binding policy | the cell that the input's source names |
| Which answer in that cell | the answer the decider's policy names (`source.reduction`) | the reduction that declares it implements the input |
| Who maps question to answer | the deciding authority, in its `UITVOERINGSBELEID` | the keeper, in its reduction |

The second route has no policy of the decider in between, because the decider has no discretion over where register data comes from. The mapping from the input to a reduction is still a choice, and arguably a policy choice. It is the keeper's choice, though, and it lives with the keeper's reduction.

### 3. What is recorded and what the law asks are different things

A law that asks for a value and a law that says what is recorded do different jobs, and this RFC keeps them apart.

- **The law that records** says what is recorded and by whom. A permit is a decision: Wet op de accijns art. 42 says what the application holds, art. 43 lid 1 that the inspecteur grants it ("wordt door de inspecteur toegewezen"), and art. 48 when the inspecteur may revoke it. Those articles are where the decisions are taken ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)), and the inspecteur's chronicle holds them ([RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046)). A record that is not a decision, such as an entry in the BRP or the handelsregister, is a fact that the register law requires, with its fields and the authority that establishes it (`requires`, `fact`, `competent_authority`, [RFC-051](/rfcs/rfc-051)). It sits in the register law, the Wet BRP or the Handelsregisterwet 2007, never in the law that consumes it.
- **The law that asks** has only an input. Its source names the competent authority and passes the parameters of the question:

```yaml
# wet_op_de_accijns/accijnsplicht_alcohol, art. 39, proposed
machine_readable:
  execution:
    parameters:
      - name: kvk_nummer
        type: string
    input:
      - name: heeft_agp_vergunning_register
        type: boolean
        source:
          competent_authority:
            name: de inspecteur
          parameters: {kvk_nummer: $kvk_nummer}
    output:
      - name: heeft_agp_vergunning
        type: boolean
```

The reduction bridges the two. It reads what was recorded, in the shape the recording law gave it, and answers what the consuming law asks, in the shape the input declares. Whether a permit holds on a day is not what the inspecteur records; it follows from a grant and possibly a later revocation.

Other articles of the same law, and other laws, read the value as an output of this article through `source.regulation` ([RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007)). Art. 5 of the same rulework already does so for `heeft_agp_vergunning`.

### 4. The keeper's reduction implements the input

A reduction says which input of which law it answers, with `implements`, in the shape that `open_terms` and `implements` already have between a law and its policy ([inversion of control](/concepts/inversion-of-control)):

```yaml
# corpus/cells/<inspecteur>/reductions/reductie_agp_vergunningen, shortened
regulatory_layer: REDUCTIE
implements:
  - law: wet_op_de_accijns
    article: '39'
    input: heeft_agp_vergunning_register
articles:
  - number: '1'
    machine_readable:
      execution:
        parameters: [kvk_nummer, peildatum]
        input:
          # Grants that still hold after objection and appeal.
          - name: verleningen
            type: array
            source:
              regulation: reductie_algemene_wet_bestuursrecht
              output: geldende_besluiten
              parameters: {basis: wet_op_de_accijns#43}
          - name: intrekkingen
            type: array
            source:
              regulation: reductie_algemene_wet_bestuursrecht
              output: geldende_besluiten
              parameters: {basis: wet_op_de_accijns#48}
        output:
          - name: heeft_agp_vergunning_register
            type: boolean
```

The reduction answers with the output that carries the name of the input it implements. It reads the decisions with the means a reduction has today: `source.decisions` gives one row per decision under an article, with its fields, findings and outputs, and the Awb reduction of the PoC (`reductie_algemene_wet_bestuursrecht`) narrows those to the decisions that still hold (RFC-048 §1). A revocation under art. 48 names the grant it revokes in a field of type `decision`, which holds the id of that decision (RFC-045 §6), so the reduction can pair them. The permit holds on the day asked when a grant for that KVK number holds and no revocation of it took effect on or before that day.

For this to work, art. 42 and 43 have to be modelled as an application and its decision (`produces`, RFC-045). The demo rulework does not do that today: it models art. 39 alone, with a `source: {}` input. Art. 48 adds a gap of its own. The cell records a decision on a submission, and a revocation at the holder's request (onder f) has one. The other grounds of art. 48 are decisions the inspecteur takes of his own accord, without an application, and the cell cannot record those yet. The PoC already runs into this: its parking-permit reduction says that a revocation is not in the chronicle as a decision.

A register entry that is not a decision is read the same way, with `source.facts` on the fact the register law requires. The KVK's reduction reads `handelsregisterwet_2007#<article>#<fact>` and implements the input of whichever consuming law asks.

### 5. Naming the authority, or reading from the register law

An input names a role, never a cell (RFC-051 §4). There are two ways to name it.

- **The consuming law names the authority itself.** Art. 39 says "een vergunning ... verstrekt door de inspecteur". The input then names the inspecteur in `source.competent_authority`, because that is what the article says.
- **The competence comes from another law.** The Wet op de accijns does not say who issues the KVK number; the Handelsregisterwet 2007 does (art. 9 onder a). A value like that is read from the register law, through `source.regulation` from the article that establishes it, as RFC-051 §4 requires. The register law's article is the one whose input names the Kamer, and the Kamer's reduction implements that input.

The proposal is that an input may name a `competent_authority` only where the text of its own article names the authority. Where the competence lies in a register law that is not in the corpus yet, the input stays `source: {}` and is supplied by hand until that law is added. Naming the authority anyway would state a competence the article does not contain, and would have to be undone once the register law arrives. Whether a provisional `competent_authority` with a `legal_basis` pointing at the other law is acceptable instead is an open question.

`competent_authority` on an input source has the shape of RFC-002 and RFC-051: a named authority or a list, with `INSTANCE` matched by name and `CATEGORY` by category. RFC-051 puts it on an open term (who may answer the term) and on a fact (who establishes it). This RFC adds a third place with a third meaning: who answers this input from its own records. Where an input's answer comes from a fact, both carry an authority: the fact in the register law says who records, the input in the consuming law says who answers. They name the same authority for the same act; §11 holds the cell against the input's, and RFC-051 already restricts who records the fact.

### 6. The key travels as a parameter

The `with key "kvk_nummer"` of a data table becomes a parameter of the question: the input passes `parameters: {kvk_nummer: $kvk_nummer}`, and the reduction declares `kvk_nummer` as a parameter. The engine hands over the calculation date as `peildatum`.

A key is often itself a value a keeper establishes. The law that makes the Kamer keep the register also makes it the issuer of the number (Handelsregisterwet 2007 art. 9 onder a, for a company, and art. 11 lid 1 onder a, for an establishment). A citizen's BSN is issued under the Wet algemene bepalingen burgerservicenummer. The key is not a parameter the case invents.

The key does not have to come from a register either. A caller may pass a KVK number by hand, and the law does not ask where it came from.

### 7. The cell is one way to supply a value, never the only one

A law stays executable on its own. Every input that names a competent authority can still be supplied by hand: as a parameter, from a data table or a data source of the call, or by a simulation. `competent_authority` on the source says who is competent by law to answer. It is information about the law, and the engine does not read it as a rule that the value must come from that cell.

So the engine resolves such an input as follows:

| Supplied by the caller | Competent cell in the call | Result |
|---|---|---|
| yes | no | the supplied value |
| no | yes | the answer of the cell |
| yes | yes | an error: the engine does not choose between two sources for one value |
| no | no | the input has no value, as a `source: {}` input without data has none today ([RFC-036](/rfcs/rfc-036) decides what that means) |

The third row follows RFC-048 §2, where a finding on a term that a policy fills makes the execution fail rather than letting one silently win. A caller that wants a cell to answer leaves the value out, and a caller that supplies the value leaves the cell out. A supplied value does not take precedence over the cell either, as a correction by hand would: that would be a priority rule of the engine's own, which RFC-048 avoids.

Recording stays restricted and consuming does not. A cell records a fact only when the fact names that organization (RFC-051 §2), and a decision only under an article it is competent for; those rules are unchanged. Who supplies the value to a consuming law is not restricted, because the consumer has no way to verify it anyway and the law does not require it.

The trace and the execution result say where each such value came from. An input resolved from the caller's data is marked as supplied, with the source it came from. An input a cell answered is marked as answered, with the cell, the reduction and its version, and how far the reduction read the chronicle, as for an open term (RFC-048 §5). This sits next to the provenance of outputs (direct, reactive or override, which the Gherkin steps already assert), and it is about inputs.

A production caller may want the opposite: that a value of another authority is never taken from hand-supplied data. That is the authoritative mode of [RFC-009](/rfcs/rfc-009) §3, in which the engine accepts a value for which another organization is the competent authority from that organization, and errors rather than computing or guessing it. This RFC extends that mode to these inputs: in authoritative mode, an input whose source names a `competent_authority` other than the engine's own identity is answered by the competent cell, and a value the caller supplies for it is an error. Solo simulation, the default of RFC-009, ignores `competent_authority` and keeps the table above. The base registries already have this shape in law: Wet BRP art. 1.7 lid 1 and Handelsregisterwet 2007 art. 30 lid 1 oblige an administrative body to use the registry value, with an exception after a correction report (lid 2 onder b of both).

### 8. One question goes to one cell

An input may name more than one competent authority. The BRP is the clearest case: the college of each municipality keeps the entries of its residents, and the minister those of non-residents (Wet BRP art. 1.4). Several cells can then answer the same input, but a single question always goes to one specific cell, such as the municipality where the person is registered or the minister. That is the rule of RFC-048 §4, and this route follows it.

Which competent cell is asked must therefore be derivable from something in the case, such as the person's municipality of registration on the day asked about. How the engine derives it is not designed yet (see open questions). Until it is, a call that holds more than one cell that the input names is an error.

### 9. Keepers outside the administration

A keeper need not be an administrative body. A pension fund, a health insurer or a notary keeps records a law relies on.

- **When a law designates the keeper**, the input (or, in the register law, the fact) names it in `competent_authority`, as for any keeper. RFC-051 does not restrict `competent_authority` to administrative bodies, and this route treats such a keeper like any other cell.
- **When only a policy designates the keeper**, for example a decider that takes a pension fund's statement of accrued rights, the law names no authority for the input. The value is then an open term, and the decider's `UITVOERINGSBELEID` binds it to the keeper's named answer (`source.reduction`), the route of RFC-048 §2. That route asks the cell that the filled term names, or else the policy's holder (RFC-048 §4). For a keeper that the law's term does not name, the binding has to say which cell it asks, which RFC-048 does not provide yet.

### 10. Hand-supplied and chronicle scenarios

Both kinds of scenario are first-class. Neither replaces the other.

- **Hand-supplied scenarios** test a rulework on its own. They give values with data tables and parameters, and open terms with `findings`. That is how most of the corpus is tested, and it stays so: the law can be read and checked without any cell.
- **Chronicle scenarios** test the chain end to end. They record grams in the cells, and evaluate with those cells in the call, so the keepers' real reductions answer. A chronicle scenario needs no mock reductions. Where a test wants to stand in for a cell, it supplies the value by hand, which is what a data table or a finding already does.

A scenario may mix the two: one input answered by a cell, another supplied in a table. Only the same input from both sources is an error (§7).

### 11. What is declared must fit, what is missing is allowed

The coupling between a law, its input and a cell is checked statically, wherever a cell or a reduction is present. This is a consistency check on what the corpus declares. It does not restrict at run time where a value may come from (§7). A corpus that breaks one of these rules fails validation:

1. A reduction that implements an input names an input that exists on that article of the law, has a source that names a `competent_authority`, and is in force somewhere in the reduction's own validity.
2. The cell that keeps the reduction matches that `competent_authority` with its identity in `cel.yaml`: an `INSTANCE` by name, a `CATEGORY` by category, as the engine matches answerers today (RFC-048 §4). A cell that the input does not name cannot claim it.
3. The answer fits the input. The reduction declares an output under the input's name, and that output has the input's type, its unit (`type_spec`) and no wider nullability. For `source.regulation` the type checker holds nullability against the target today (N5). The same check applies here, and type and unit are compared as well.
4. A consuming law's `source.regulation` names an output that exists. This is enforced already: `script/cross-law-integriteit.py` reports such a binding as `DANGLING`, and CI runs it over `corpus/regulation` and the chronolexografie corpus, though not over `corpus/demo`.

Two things are deliberately not checked:

- That every input with a `competent_authority` has a cell that implements it. A law must run without cells, so an input nobody implements is allowed. A coverage report may list them, as information.
- That a supplied value came from the competent cell (§7).

The first rule follows the same path as an `implements` on an open term, which the cross-law gate already checks (`IMPL-DANGLING`): the gate learns a second kind of target. The second rule needs the cells, so it belongs where a corpus with cells is loaded, next to the check that no reduction calls one of another cell (RFC-048 §3). What a reduction reads from the chronicle (`source.decisions`, `source.facts`) is checked as RFC-048 §3 and RFC-051 already do.

### What this changes in RFC-048 and RFC-051

- RFC-048 §1 says a reduction fills no open term and has no `implements`. A reduction still fills no open term. It may now carry `implements` entries with an `input` and no `open_term`.
- RFC-048 §2, where the decider's policy binds a term to an answer, stays as it is for open terms. For an input whose source names a competent authority, the law decides who answers, and no binding sits in between.
- RFC-048 §3 lets only a reduction read a chronicle. That stays: the input asks a cell a question, and only the cell's reduction reads the chronicle.
- RFC-051 §1 puts a fact in the law that requires it. That stays, and this RFC adds where that is: in the register law, not in the law that consumes the value.
- RFC-051 §2 says a term that reads a fact must repeat the fact's competent authorities, because the engine does not link the term to the fact. That rule stays for open terms. An input on this route carries its own `competent_authority`, so there is nothing to repeat and nothing for the engine to link.
- RFC-051 §4 says the law reads an established value through `source.regulation` from the establishing article, and names a role, never a cell. Both stay (§5).

## Worked example: the AGP permit and the handelsregister

The scenario in the context checks whether a brewery with KVK number 85234567 may produce without a permit for an accijnsgoederenplaats. Today the data table states that the Douane register holds no permit.

As a chronicle scenario the same case reads:

```gherkin
Background:
  Given the calculation date is "2024-06-01"
  And parameter "kvk_nummer" is "85234567"
  And the cell "inspecteur_fictief" is in the call

Scenario: Produceren met een ingetrokken vergunning
  Given the cell "inspecteur_fictief" decides under "wet_op_de_accijns#43" on "2022-02-01":
    | kvk_nummer | 85234567 |
  And the cell "inspecteur_fictief" decides under "wet_op_de_accijns#48" on "2024-03-15":
    | vergunning | the decision of 2022-02-01 |
  And parameter "activiteit" is "productie"
  When I evaluate outputs "heeft_agp_vergunning, voldoet_aan_vergunningsplicht" of "wet_op_de_accijns/accijnsplicht_alcohol"
  Then output "heeft_agp_vergunning" is false
  And output "voldoet_aan_vergunningsplicht" is false
  And input "heeft_agp_vergunning_register" was answered by cell "inspecteur_fictief"
```

The two `decides` steps are shorthand for an application and the decision on it, which the cell records as two chronolexograms each (RFC-046); how a scenario refers to an earlier decision is left to the implementation. The engine reaches art. 39 and finds an input whose source names the inspecteur. The cell `inspecteur_fictief` matches it. The cell finds the reduction that implements the input, runs it on its chronicle with `kvk_nummer` and the calculation date, and answers false: the grant of 2022 was revoked under art. 48 before 1 June 2024. The trace records who answered, from which reduction, and how far it read the chronicle, as for an open term (RFC-048 §5).

The permit was never in the Douane "register" as a row. It is in the inspecteur's chronicle as two decisions, and the scenario can show a case that a single row cannot: a permit that held in 2023 and no longer holds.

The scenario from the context keeps working as it is. Its data table supplies `heeft_agp_vergunning_register` for KVK number 85234567, no cell is in the call, and the engine takes the supplied value (the first row of the table in §7). The two scenarios test different things: the hand-supplied one tests the accijns rules on their own, the chronicle one also tests the inspecteur's reduction.

The Kamer van Koophandel shows the other kind of record. An entry of an establishment is no decision. Handelsregisterwet 2007 art. 11 lid 1 lists what the register holds about it, among which "het post- en bezoekadres" (onder c), so the fact `vestiging_ingeschreven` belongs on that article, with the Kamer as its `competent_authority` (art. 3 lid 1). The PoC's reduction `reductie_vestiging_in_handelsregister` already reads that fact; it would declare `implements` for the input of the consuming law that asks for the postcode. The fictional policy `beleid_handelsregister_kamer_van_koophandel` now carries the fact because the Handelsregisterwet 2007 is not in the corpus. Once it is, the fact moves to the law, the consuming parking rulework of Wilgendaal gets an input naming the Kamer, and its policy no longer needs a binding for the postcode.

## Where a `source: {}` value can come from

Every value a law reads can be supplied by hand (§7). The question here is a different one: for which values does a cell route exist at all? A value can reach a law through a cell in three ways:

- **(a)** as a record kept by a keeper cell, a decision or a fact, which this RFC covers;
- **(b)** as a submission recorded in the deciding cell, such as a statement in an application ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045));
- **(c)** as an open term answered under RFC-048, or as the finding of the deciding authority.

Values that fit none of these have no cell route. The caller supplies them, as it does today, and that is not a defect.

We went through the 282 `source: {}` inputs in `corpus/demo/regulation` (200), `corpus/regulation` (63) and the chronolexografie PoC corpus (19), using the demo bindings to see where each value comes from.

| Route | Inputs | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| (a) register of a public authority | about 145 in the demo, most of the 62 elsewhere | BRP (RvIG, municipalities), Belastingdienst income components, KVK, UWV, SVB, DUO, DJI, Bibob advice (Landelijk Bureau Bibob) |
| (b) submission or case | 17 | huurprijs and childcare hours in an application, procedural events in Awb 7:1 and 8:1 |
| (c) open term or finding | about 21 | the Awb art. 1:1 classification of an organization (`is_rechterlijk_orgaan`, `overheidsfinanciering_percentage`), `is_ingezetene` in the Wlz, where the children live |

Some values have a cell route only with a qualification:

- **Keepers that are not administrative bodies (9 demo inputs, plus `polis_status` in the Zvw).** A pension fund, a health insurer, a notary and the Stichting Vakbekwaamheid Horeca keep records a law relies on. They are keepers like any other when a law designates them, and reach a law through a policy binding when only a policy does (§9).
- **Statistics (1 input).** The CBS life expectancy fits (a): AOW art. 7a lid 4 says the estimates "worden uitgevoerd en bekendgemaakt door het CBS". It is not a fact about a person, though. The demo and its scenarios look it up by BSN, while its real key is the year.

Other values have no cell route, and the caller supplies them:

- **The legal order itself (7 inputs).** The Awb objection and appeal articles read `wet`, `termijn_weken`, `beslistermijn_weken` and a `jurisdictie` from reference tables, and an APV reads a terrace tariff per square meter from one. These values are law or policy. In time they belong in a rulework read through `source.regulation`, which is a separate change.
- **The polling day.** The Kieswet reads `verkiezingsdatum` as `source: {}`. It is no fact about the voter but part of what is asked: whether someone may vote in this election.
- **Engine fixtures (20 inputs).** The `test_null_semantics`, `test_nullable` and `test_scoped_source_bron` ruleworks test how data sources behave, and stay as they are.

The calculation date is not among the inputs at all. It is part of the question, the day the caller asks about, and the engine hands it to a reduction as `peildatum`. The alcohol percentage and the quantity in the accijns case are parameters of the declaration, so (b).

Most register values have a cell route. Most of the rest are submissions or findings, and what remains is part of the question or of the law.

## Why

### Benefits

- A register value gets provenance: who recorded it, on which day, and on what legal basis. A data table has none of that.
- The law stays in its own terms. It says that the inspecteur grants the permit, which is what art. 39 says, and the format needs no word for register or cell.
- The consuming law declares nothing twice. It has an input and the authority that answers it; what is recorded lives in the law that governs the recording.
- No fictional policy of the decider is needed where the decider has no choice. The PoC's binding for the KVK postcode disappears.
- Hand-supplied scenarios and the demo bindings keep working unchanged (§7), so moving an input from `source: {}` to a named authority does not have to wait for its cells.
- A scenario can express time: a permit granted and revoked, an address that changed. A row holds one state.

### Tradeoffs

- The keeper's mapping from input to reduction is a choice that nobody outside the cell reviews, unlike a published policy.
- An input only gets a cell route once its keeper has a cell with a reduction. For the demo that is roughly forty services, many with several tables. Until then the input is supplied by hand, as today.
- A permit only reaches a reduction as a decision once the articles that grant and revoke it are modelled as decisions. For the accijns case that is art. 42, 43 and 48, which the demo does not model.
- Outside the authoritative mode the engine accepts a supplied value (§7), so it cannot tell a deliberate hand-supplied value from a cell someone forgot to put in the call. The provenance in the trace shows which happened, but only the authoritative mode refuses it.
- An input naming an authority and an open term are two routes to a cell, and an author has to choose between them per input. The rule of thumb is discretion: if the deciding authority chooses where the value comes from, it is an open term.

### Alternatives Considered

**A policy binding for every register value, as in the PoC.**
- Every register value would become an open term with a `UITVOERINGSBELEID` of the decider binding it to an answer.
- Rejected: the decider has no discretion, so the policy is fiction. It also makes every consuming authority write the same binding again for the same register.

**The consuming law declares the fact it reads.**
- The first draft of this RFC had art. 39 declare the fact under `requires` and read it with a `source.fact` naming that fact.
- Rejected: the fact was declared twice in the consuming law, and its `parameters` were the fields of the record, which is not what the law asks. A permit is a decision of the inspecteur under art. 43, not a fact of art. 39.

**The keeper's catalogue maps the input.**
- `cel.yaml` would list per input which reduction answers it, as it lists named answers today.
- Rejected for now: the catalogue holds no logic, and `implements` on the reduction keeps the mapping next to the code that answers and in the same shape as policy implementing a law. The catalogue can still be derived from it (see implementation).

**The law names the reduction or the cell.**
- The input's source would name a reduction or a cell directly.
- Rejected: RFC-051 §4. The law names a role, and which organization fills the role can differ per case and over time.

**Keep data sources.**
- `source: {}` stays and the cell writes a table that the data source reads.
- Rejected: the table loses the dated acts behind it, which is what the cell adds.

## Implementation sketch

None of this is built.

- **Schema (v0.8.14).** `competent_authority` on an input's `source`, in the shape of RFC-002, with `parameters` beside it and no other key. An `implements` entry with `law`, `article` and `input`, allowed only on a `REDUCTIE`, and on a `REDUCTIE` only with `input`. The rest of RFC-048 §3 stays.
- **Checks.** The rules of §11 extend what exists rather than adding a checker next to it: rule 1 goes into `script/cross-law-integriteit.py` beside `IMPL-DANGLING`, rule 3 into the type checker (`typecheck::check_input_sources`), and rule 2 into the loading of a corpus with cells. The cross-law gate should then also run over `corpus/demo`.
- **Engine.** A new question kind next to `AnswerRequest`: the law, article and input, the parameters, and the calculation date. `OpenTermAnswerer` gets a method for it, or a second trait does. Resolution follows the table in §7: look for a supplied value in the data sources of the call, and for answerers in the call that the input's `competent_authority` names. The trace and `ArticleResult` record per input whether it was supplied or answered, and by whom. In the authoritative mode of RFC-009 a supplied value for an input of another authority is an error; that mode is not built yet, so this part waits for it.
- **Cell.** The cell indexes its reductions by `implements` when it opens, refuses two reductions that implement the same input, and answers such a question by running the reduction that implements it. `cel.yaml` keeps its `answers` for open terms. For inputs it needs no entry, and `Cell::answers` can list the derived ones next to the declared ones.
- **Corpus.** The articles that grant and revoke a permit become decisions (`produces`); facts that are no decision go into the register law (`requires`, `fact`). For the worked example that is Wet op de accijns art. 42, 43 and 48, and Handelsregisterwet 2007 art. 11.
- **Gherkin.** New steps in `bdd/grammar.yaml`, in a tier of their own: a cell is in the call, a cell records a fact on a day (a vertical table of the fact's parameters), and a cell decides on an application under an article on a day. Next to the output provenance steps comes an assertion on an input: that it was answered by a given cell, or supplied. The Rust runner in `packages/engine/tests/bdd` needs `regelrecht-cel` as a dev-dependency. Cargo accepts that cycle for integration tests, since `regelrecht-cel` depends on the engine. The JS runner in `packages/frontend-shared` drives the engine's WASM build, which has no cell. Until it does, it should skip such a scenario with a stated reason rather than fail. `docs/scripts/check-gherkin-steps.mjs` and the demo's Gherkin templates read the grammar too.
- **Where demo cells live.** `corpus/demo/cells/<cell>/`, next to `corpus/demo/regulation/`, like the PoC.
- **Docs.** The glossary entries for reduction and `REDUCTIE`, the [chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography) page and [testing](/guide/testing) describe the second route and the chronicle scenarios.

## Open questions

- **Which competent cell is asked.** Where an input names several competent authorities, such as the municipalities and the minister for the BRP, one question goes to one specific cell (§8). From what the engine derives which one (a parameter of the question, an earlier fact, the keeper's own chronicle) is not designed. The minister is responsible for most provision of data from the BRP (art. 1.5 lid 1), which suggests one answering cell holding copies ([RFC-053](/rfcs/rfc-053)), but that would make the minister answer for what the municipalities record.
- **A register law that is not in the corpus.** §5 proposes that an input names an authority only where its own article does, and otherwise stays `source: {}` until the register law is added. The alternative is a provisional `competent_authority` with a `legal_basis` pointing at the register law.
- **A keeper that only a policy names.** The binding of RFC-048 asks the cell the filled term names, or the policy's holder (§9). A binding that asks a keeper neither names needs a way to say so.
- **A decision without an application.** A revocation the inspecteur makes of his own accord (art. 48, other than onder f) has no submission, and the cell records decisions only on submissions (§4). The worked example needs that to be possible.
- **The legal order in reference tables.** The Awb inputs that read a law, a period or a jurisdiction from a table should become rulework. That is a separate change, not part of this RFC.
