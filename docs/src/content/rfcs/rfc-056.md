## Decision

A register is a cell. An input whose authority the law names can be answered by that authority's cell, through a reduction that declares it implements the input. No policy of the deciding authority sits in between, because the decider has no say in where register data comes from.

The cell route is optional. Every value can still be supplied by hand.

## Why

Most values a law reads about a person or a company come from a register, written today as `source: {}`. A data table or `corpus/demo/bindings.yaml` then supplies the value, without saying who recorded it, when, or on what basis.

A cell can say that ([RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046)), but today it only answers open terms, bound by the decider's policy ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048)). The chronolexografie PoC needs a fictional municipal policy just to reach the postcode in the handelsregister. That policy expresses no discretion, because there is none.

## The register law records, the consuming law asks

What is recorded and what a law asks are different things. A reduction bridges them.

The register law says what is recorded and by whom. A permit is a decision: Wet op de accijns art. 42 lists what the application holds, art. 43 lid 1 has the inspecteur grant it ("wordt door de inspecteur toegewezen"), and art. 48 lets him revoke it. A record that is no decision, such as an entry in the BRP or the handelsregister, is a fact the register law requires (`requires`, `fact`, `competent_authority`, [RFC-051](/rfcs/rfc-051)).

The consuming law has only an input. Its source names the competent authority and passes the parameters of the question:

```yaml
# wet_op_de_accijns/accijnsplicht_alcohol, art. 39, proposed
input:
  - name: heeft_agp_vergunning_register
    type: boolean
    source:
      competent_authority:
        name: de inspecteur
      parameters: {kvk_nummer: $kvk_nummer}
```

The key of a data table (`with key "kvk_nummer"`) becomes such a parameter. Other articles and laws keep reading the value through `source.regulation` from this article ([RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007)).

## The keeper's reduction implements the input

A reduction names the input it answers, in the shape `open_terms` and `implements` already have ([inversion of control](/concepts/inversion-of-control)):

```yaml
# cells/<inspecteur>/reductions/reductie_agp_vergunningen, shortened
regulatory_layer: REDUCTIE
articles:
  - number: '1'
    machine_readable:
      implements:
        - {law: wet_op_de_accijns, article: '39', input: heeft_agp_vergunning_register}
      execution:
        parameters: [kvk_nummer, peildatum]
        input:
          - name: verleningen
            source:
              regulation: reductie_algemene_wet_bestuursrecht
              output: geldende_besluiten
              parameters: {basis: wet_op_de_accijns#43}
          - name: intrekkingen
            source:
              regulation: reductie_algemene_wet_bestuursrecht
              output: geldende_besluiten
              parameters: {basis: wet_op_de_accijns#48}
        output:
          - name: heeft_agp_vergunning_register
            type: boolean
```

`implements` sits on the article, as it does for policy. The answer is the output with the input's name. The engine passes the calculation date as `peildatum`.

The reduction reads with what exists today. `source.decisions` gives one row per decision, and the PoC's Awb reduction keeps those that still hold after objection and appeal. The application and the grant sit on one article, art. 43, as in the Leerplichtwet and the BABW: a cell records an application only on an article with outputs, so art. 42 is not modelled on its own. A revocation names the grant it revokes in a field of type `decision` (RFC-045 §6). A fact that is no decision is read with `source.facts`.

## An input names an authority only where its article does

Art. 39 names the inspecteur, so its input may too. The law names a role, never a cell (RFC-051 §4).

Where the competence lies in another law, the input reads from that law with `source.regulation`. The KVK number is an example: Handelsregisterwet 2007 art. 9 onder a makes the Kamer its issuer. Until the register law is in the corpus, such an input stays `source: {}` and is supplied by hand.

This is a third place for `competent_authority`, in the shape of RFC-002. On an open term it says who may answer, on a fact who records, and on an input source who answers from its own records.

## The cell is optional

A law stays executable on its own. `competent_authority` on an input is information about the law, not a rule that the value must come from that cell. Recording stays restricted to the competent cell (RFC-051 §2); supplying a value to a consuming law does not.

The engine resolves such an input in this order:

1. A value supplied by the caller and an answer from a competent cell: error. The engine does not choose between two sources.
2. A supplied value and no answer: that value. A named cell without a reduction for this input stays silent, so the supplied value counts.
3. An answer and no supplied value: the answer.
4. Neither: no value, as for `source: {}` today ([RFC-036](/rfcs/rfc-036)).

The trace marks each such input as supplied (with its source) or answered (with the cell, the reduction, its version and how far it read), beside the existing output provenance.

In the authoritative mode of [RFC-009](/rfcs/rfc-009) §3 the cell is required: a supplied value for another authority's input is an error. Solo simulation, the default, follows the order above. Wet BRP art. 1.7 and Handelsregisterwet 2007 art. 30 already oblige an administrative body to use the registry value.

## One question goes to one cell

An input may name several authorities: the municipalities and the minister keep the BRP (Wet BRP art. 1.4). Each question still goes to one specific cell, as in RFC-048 §4. Until the engine can derive which one, two matching cells in a call are an error.

## Keepers outside the administration

A pension fund, insurer or notary is a keeper like any other when a law names it. When only a policy names it, the value is an open term and the decider's policy binds it (RFC-048 §2).

## What is declared must fit

Where a cell or a reduction is present, validation checks:

1. The implemented input exists on that article, in some version of the law, and names a `competent_authority`.
2. The cell's identity in `cel.yaml` matches that authority (`INSTANCE` by name, `CATEGORY` by category).
3. The reduction's output has the input's type, unit and no wider nullability.
4. A `source.regulation` points at an existing output. `script/cross-law-integriteit.py` already checks this (`DANGLING`), though CI does not run it over `corpus/demo`.

Not checked: that every such input has a cell, or that a supplied value came from one. What is missing is allowed.

## Testing

Hand-supplied scenarios (data tables, parameters, `findings`) stay first-class. They test a rulework on its own.

Chronicle scenarios record grams in cells and test the chain with real reductions. A scenario may mix both, but not for the same input.

## Worked example: a revoked permit

```gherkin
Background:
  Given the calculation date is "2024-06-01"
  And parameter "kvk_nummer" is "85234567"
  And the cell "inspecteur_fictief" is in the call

Scenario: Produceren met een ingetrokken vergunning
  Given the cell "inspecteur_fictief" decides under "wet_op_de_accijns#43" on "2022-02-01":
    | kvk_nummer | 85234567 |
  And the cell "inspecteur_fictief" decides under "wet_op_de_accijns#48" on "2024-03-15":
    | vergunning | the decision of 2022-02-01 |
  And parameter "activiteit" is "productie"
  When I evaluate outputs "heeft_agp_vergunning, voldoet_aan_vergunningsplicht" of "wet_op_de_accijns/accijnsplicht_alcohol"
  Then output "heeft_agp_vergunning" is false
  And output "voldoet_aan_vergunningsplicht" is false
  And input "heeft_agp_vergunning_register" was answered by cell "inspecteur_fictief"
```

The engine reaches art. 39 and finds the inspecteur named. The matching cell runs its reduction and answers false, because the 2022 grant was revoked before 1 June 2024. A single register row could not show that history.

The existing scenario with a data table keeps passing: no cell is in the call, so the supplied value counts.

The KVK is the fact case. Its entry of an establishment (Handelsregisterwet 2007 art. 11, kept by the Kamer under art. 3 lid 1) becomes a fact in that law. The PoC reduction `reductie_vestiging_in_handelsregister` then implements the consuming input, and the fictional parking policy loses its binding.

## Which inputs have a cell route

We went through the 282 `source: {}` inputs in the demo, the corpus and the PoC. About 200 are register values with a keeper, a few of them private parties. Another 17 are submissions, and about 21 are findings or open terms.

The rest have no cell route and are supplied by the caller. That includes the legal order itself (periods, tariffs), engine fixtures and the polling day. The calculation date is part of the question, not an input.

## What changes in RFC-048 and RFC-051

- A `REDUCTIE` may carry `implements` with an `input` (RFC-048 §1 allowed none). It still fills no open term.
- An input on this route needs no binding by the decider's policy (RFC-048 §2 stays for open terms).
- A fact sits in the register law, not in the consuming law (RFC-051 §1).
- An input carries its own `competent_authority`, so nothing is repeated from a fact (RFC-051 §2 stays for open terms).

## Tradeoffs and alternatives

- Tradeoff: the keeper's mapping from input to reduction is not reviewed as a published policy is.
- Tradeoff: outside authoritative mode, a forgotten cell looks like a hand-supplied value. Only the trace tells them apart.
- Tradeoff: a permit only reaches a reduction once art. 43 and 48 are modelled as decisions.
- Rejected: a policy binding per register value, as in the PoC. It is fiction where the decider has no discretion.
- Rejected: the consuming law declares the fact and reads it (`source.fact`). It declares the fact twice and confuses the record with the question.
- Rejected: a provisional `competent_authority` that cites the register law in `legal_basis`. It claims a competence the article does not state.

## To build

- Schema v0.8.14: `competent_authority` with `parameters` on an input source; `implements` with `input` on an article of a `REDUCTIE` only; a `/` allowed in the law id of an article reference, since demo `$id`s contain one.
- Checks: rule 1 beside `IMPL-DANGLING` in the cross-law gate, rule 3 in `typecheck::check_input_sources`, rule 2 in a corpus loader that opens the cells with a service, which does not exist outside the PoC yet; the gate also over `corpus/demo`.
- Engine: a question kind beside `AnswerRequest`, the resolution order above, provenance per input, and the authoritative-mode error.
- Cell: index reductions by `implements`, refuse two for one input, and answer by running the one that matches.
- Gherkin: steps for a cell in the call, recording a fact, deciding under an article, and asserting who answered an input. The Rust runner takes `regelrecht-cel` as a dev-dependency; the JS runner skips these steps until the WASM build carries a cell.

## Open questions

- From what does the engine derive which of several competent cells to ask?
- How does a cell record a decision taken without an application, such as most revocations under art. 48?
- How does a policy point at a private keeper that is neither the term's named cell nor its holder? Left open until a real case exists.
