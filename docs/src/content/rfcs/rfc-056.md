## Context

A reduction reads what a cell recorded and gives a named answer ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048)). Today it does so as a query. It takes the rows of `source.decisions` or `source.facts`, filters them with a `FOREACH`, and combines what is left with `combine`, such as `OR` or `ONLY`.

A reviewer of the chronolexography PoC pointed out that a reduction is closer to a read model in event sourcing. It replays the events in the order they happened and keeps a state that each event changes. In mathematical terms that is a fold, or reduce, over the ordered list of chronolexograms that matter for the question, as `Array.prototype.reduce` folds an array. [RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022) §4.1 already names the axis: the made-it-fact timestamp every chronolexogram has.

The query form shows its limits in three places, each an [open question](/concepts/chronolexography/open-questions):

- The reduction of the Awb computes the decisions that still hold with sets: the decisions under an article, minus those revoked on objection (Awb 7:11), minus those the court annulled (8:72). Each set is an intermediate output with a list of ids, because inside a `FOREACH` the binding of an outer `FOREACH` cannot be read. The order of events plays no part, although the law describes a sequence: a decision is taken, then reconsidered, then judged.
- `combine: ONLY` refuses two records that both qualify. A correction recorded on the same day then blocks the decision, and a chronicle never removes the first record.
- The fictitious parking ordinance of Wilgendaal lets the college revoke a business parking permit (article 5). No reduction read a revocation, so a revoked permit kept counting until it expired.

## Decision

### 1. `FOLD` plays a collection through

`FOLD` (schema v0.8.14) starts from a value and gives, for each element in the order of the collection, the next state. The result is the state after the last element.

```yaml
operation: FOLD
collection: $kroniek          # the rows, in their order
as: rij                       # the current element
state: geldend                # the state so far
initial:                      # the state before the first element
  operation: LIST
  items: []
body:                         # the next state
  operation: IF
  cases:
    - when: {operation: EQUALS, subject: $rij.basis, value: $basis}
      then:
        operation: ADD
        values: [$geldend, {operation: LIST, items: [$rij]}]
  default: $geldend
```

- `collection` and `initial` are evaluated where the `FOLD` stands. A null collection plays nothing and gives `initial`; a single value is one element, as for `FOREACH`.
- `as`, `state`, `initial` and `body` are required, and `as` differs from `state`. There is no form without `as`: the fields of an element are read as `$rij.<field>` ([RFC-049](/rfcs/rfc-049)).
- Both names stay visible in operations nested in `body`, a `FOREACH` among them, unless such an operation binds the same name. That is the one point where `FOLD` departs from the scope rule of [RFC-016](/rfcs/rfc-016), under which an inner `FOREACH` body sees only its own element. A body has to drop the element from the state, or look up the decision a row refers to, and both need a `FOREACH` that reads `$rij`. The rule for `FOREACH` itself does not change.
- An untranslatable collection, start value or state ends the fold with that value. An unknown collection makes the result unknown. An error in one step ends the fold: a state played through part of the chronicle is not the answer.

The engine builds it on the machinery of `FOREACH`, with one trace step per element.

### 2. The chronicle comes in its order

A fold needs one list in which every event that matters stands in its place. `source.chronicle` (schema v0.8.14) gives the rows of several reads in one list:

```yaml
- name: kroniek
  type: array
  source:
    chronicle:
      - decisions: $basis
      - decisions: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#7:11
      - facts: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#8:79#afschrift_van_de_uitspraak
```

Each entry reads what `decisions` or `facts` reads on its own, and its rows have the same shape. A chronolexogram that two entries read comes once.

The order is the order in which things became fact in the chronicle. That is the day the cell recorded them (`recorded_on`), and within one day the order in which the cell recorded them. The second key is deterministic because a chronicle only appends, refuses a record dated before the last one, and keeps its file in order of appending. The rows of a plain `source.decisions` or `source.facts` come in the same order; the schema now says so.

The tie-break within a day is the order of recording, not an order in law. Two records of one day that the law would order otherwise, such as a decision and its correction made known later that day, come in the order the cell recorded them. The open question on a second date for the moment that counts in law still stands.

### 3. The reduction of the Awb is a fold

The reduction of the Awb folds over the decisions under the article it is given, the decisions on objection (7:11) and the receipts of judgments (8:79). Its state is the list of decisions that hold, and each row changes it:

- a decision under the article holds from then on;
- a decision on objection that revokes the contested decision drops it;
- a judgment that annuls a decision with its legal effects drops it. When the annulled decision was a decision on objection, the decision it took in place drops too, and the decision it revoked holds again, unless the judgment takes the place of the decision on objection and revokes it itself (8:72 lid 3 onder b);
- what the court set in place holds from then on.

The reduction had two articles and six outputs. It now has one article and one output, `geldende_besluiten`, and the reductions that call it read that output as before. The tests in `packages/engine/tests/reduction.rs` play every chronicle through the fold and through the query it replaces, kept as a fixture, and require the same decisions to hold. The order of the list may differ: a decision that holds again comes after the judgment that revived it.

### 4. A revocation on request is a decision in the chronicle

Article 5, under a, of the fictitious parking ordinance is now executable. The holder asks for the revocation in an application that refers to the permit, and the college decides on it as on any application. Whether it revokes is a finding per case, because the ordinance says "kan intrekken", and only a permit that was granted can be revoked. Whether the request comes from the holder is not checked, because the cell does not record who submits. The reduction of the municipality calls the reduction of the Awb twice, for article 2 and for article 5, and leaves out a permit that a decision under article 5 that holds revoked. An objection against the revocation that succeeds therefore revives the permit, through the same reduction of the Awb.

That objection needed one change in the cell. On an objection the Awb calls the contested decision anew, and the revocation holds the permit by its id. The cell now passes the contested decision with every decision it refers to as a decision value, as it already did for a copy to the court (RFC-053).

That answers the open question for one route: the rule sits in the ordinance that grants the power, and the municipality's own reduction reads it. Under b, the college revokes on its own initiative, without an application. The cell takes decisions only on a submission, so that route is still open.

## Why

### Benefits

- The order of events counts where the law describes a sequence. A decision revived by a judgment follows from the place of each row, without lists of ids passed between outputs, and the reduction reads as a replay of the case file, the way a civil servant reads one.
- A later record can replace an earlier one: the state after the last record is the answer. That makes "the last advice counts" writable in a reduction, without `ONLY` and its refusal of two records.

### Tradeoffs

- The body of the Awb fold is long, and it looks up the decision on objection a judgment refers to with a `FOREACH` per lookup. For one person's case file that is small; for a whole chronicle it is quadratic. Partitioning is the remedy (below).
- The scope of `FOLD` differs from that of `FOREACH`. An author who nests a `FOREACH` in a fold sees `$rij`; in a nested `FOREACH` alone the outer element is still out of sight.
- Tests that read the intermediate outputs of the old reduction (`herroepen_besluiten`, `besluiten_in_stand`) now read `geldende_besluiten` and the trace of the fold.

### Alternatives Considered

**Operations to pick one record, such as `LATEST` or `FIND`.** They solve the correction under `ONLY` but not the Awb: dropping and reviving decisions needs a state. A fold also takes the latest record.

**Lexical scope for every `FOREACH`.** The fold would then need no exception. It changes what existing nested loops read, and RFC-016 chose the empty child scope on purpose. The exception stays with the new operation.

**A state of several named fields.** The law format has no object to build, and adding one is a larger change than a list as the state.

**Sorting in the reduction.** An operation that sorts rows by date leaves the tie-break to each reduction. The cell knows the order of its own chronicle, so it gives it.

**Who a fact is about, in the law.** A field such as `about: [{role, identified_by}]` on a decision or a fact would let a cell index its chronicle. It is rejected: the kinds of identifier and the roles are not known in advance. A key is sometimes a name and an address (the parent under Leerplichtwet 1969 art. 13a, the one who objects under Awb 6:5), sometimes an object such as a licence plate, and one person can hold several roles, such as applicant and later holder. The key belongs to the question, and the question already has it: the reduction's parameter, such as `bsn_jongere`, and the condition `$besluit.bsn_jongere == $bsn_jongere`. Selecting on that key and folding is map and reduce, without a change to the law.

## Follow-up

- **Partitioning.** A reduction now reads the whole chronicle and filters in its body. The direction is to lift the key condition from the body to the source, for example `source: {regulation: reductie_algemene_wet_bestuursrecht, output: geldende_besluiten, parameters: {basis: leerplichtwet_1969#13a}, where: {bsn_jongere: $bsn_jongere}}`. The cell then sees the key and can index on it. This is the [open question](/concepts/chronolexography/open-questions) on a filter on a source.
- **Projections kept up to date.** A fold can be kept as a projection and moved on by each new record, instead of replayed for each question. That is an optimization and needs a replay that gives the same answer.
- **The last finding wins.** The municipality of Wilgendaal now records the advice of the GGD with four medical judgments under the applicant's BSN. A municipal officer could instead read the examination, establish whether the citizen has a limitation that gives a right to a card, and record that as a fact in the municipality's own chronicle, with the medical content outside the record. The reduction then plays the findings about this person through, and the last one wins. That also settles the correction under `combine: ONLY`. This is an illustration; the corpus does not change in this RFC.

## Implementation

| Schema | Adds |
|---|---|
| v0.8.14 | `FOLD` with `collection`, `as`, `state`, `initial` and `body`; `source.chronicle`; the order of the rows of a chronicle |

The law model has `ActionOperation::Fold`, `Source::chronicle` with `ChronicleEntry`, and `Source::chronicle_reads`. The engine has `execute_fold` beside `execute_foreach`, and names a fold binds in `RuleContext::set_lexical`. The type checker, the dependency closure, the unit check and the enrichment check on undefined names know the scope of a fold. `ChronicleRead::Chronicle` carries the entries to the cell, which merges them in `reading::chronicle_rows_of`. The reading of RegelRijm has a form for `FOLD`.

The reduction of the Awb is a fold. Article 5, under a, of the fictitious parking ordinance is executable, and the reduction of the municipality reads it. A test in `packages/poc-chronolexografie/src/kern.rs` revokes a permit, objects against the revocation, and counts the permits at the next application. Not built: a decision without a submission (article 5, under b), partitioning, and projections.
