## Context

After the objection ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)) the Awb gives one more step to whoever disagrees with a decision: "Een belanghebbende kan tegen een besluit beroep instellen bij de bestuursrechter" (Awb 8:1). A party whose application was refused, and whose objection was declared unfounded, can go to court. The court then judges whether the decision on the objection stands. Its judgment is not a decision of the administrative body.

The appeal differs from the objection in two ways that matter for the model. Another body decides, and a court is not an administrative body (Awb 1:1 lid 2 onder c). The outcome differs as well. On an objection the administrative body revokes its decision and takes a new one itself (Awb 7:11 lid 2). The court annuls the decision (Awb 8:72 lid 1), and the legal effects go with it (lid 2) unless the court keeps them (lid 3 onder a). It can then order the administrative body to take a new decision within a term (lid 4).

This RFC covers the appeal in rulework and how the judgment reaches the administrative body. A cell is an organization that keeps its own chronicle of dated recordings and answers questions from it ([RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046)). The court keeps a cell of its own. A copy of the administrative body's decision goes to the court with the appeal, and a copy of the judgment comes back.

## Decision

1. **Awb 8:70 establishes the appeal and decides on it**, as 7:11 does for the objection. `produces` lists a `BEROEP` that `requests` the decision of 8:70 and holds `bestreden_besluit`, and an `UITSPRAAK` with what the judgment holds.
2. **The judgment is not a besluit**, because a court is not an administrative body (Awb 1:1 lid 2 onder c). Its kind is `UITSPRAAK`, and it follows a procedure of its own, `beroep`, with the stages BEROEP, UITSPRAAK and TOEZENDING. Handing over the copy of the judgment (8:79) is the stage TOEZENDING.
3. **The Awb hooks onto every appeal.** Awb 6:5 and 6:9 hook onto `BEROEP` as they hook onto `BEZWAAR`: the text of both speaks of the "bezwaar- of beroepschrift".
4. **An appeal is admissible** when it came in on time (6:9), when the contested decision was taken on objection (7:1 lid 1, tested as "`taken_on` is 7:11"), and when the appellant is an interested party (8:1). 8:70 does not say when an appeal is admissible; these three articles each say part of it.
5. **The court judges open norms.** A finding is what the court itself decides on an open norm in a single case. The court gives a finding per case on each of these open terms (`decided_per_case_by`):

   - whether the appellant is an interested party;
   - whether the decision breaches a rule of law;
   - which rule that is (8:77 lid 2);
   - whether the legal effects stay;
   - whether the judgment takes the place of the annulled decision;
   - the period for a new decision.

   Once the appeal is admissible, the finding on whether a rule is breached is required. The name of the rule is optional text, because a required open term cannot be empty.
6. **8:70 reads 8:72 as an input** when the appeal is well founded. 8:72 is not a hook on the judgment, because the outputs of a hook are not part of the decision value ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)). The annulment has to be an outcome of the judgment itself, or it would not reach the administrative body.
7. **Schema v0.8.10** adds `BEROEP` as a kind of submission and `UITSPRAAK` as a legal character. Nothing else.
8. **The court is a cell of its own.** The position paper has each actor keep its own facts in its own cell ("Elke actor houdt eigen feiten bij in een eigen cel"). The law also sends documents between the court and the administrative body (Awb 6:5 lid 2, 8:69 lid 1, 8:79 lid 1).

   What crosses from one cell to the other is a copy of a decision. Only the cell that took the decision makes the copy, because only that cell can take the decision again. The decision value holds no id, so the copy says what it is `about`. The receiving cell uses that to check that a judgment concerns its own decision.
9. **The administrative body records that it received the judgment.** It records this as an executogram, the recording of a fact that an article requires: here the fact `afschrift_van_de_uitspraak` that Awb 8:79 requires. The executogram holds the outcomes of 8:70 and a reference to the body's own decision. The fact names no competent authority. 8:79 says who makes the copy available (the clerk), but not who records that a party holds it.
10. **A submission that has a decision may get a new decision.** This requires a later chronolexogram that refers to the last decision on it, such as that receipt. The rule names no article. That an objection revives after an annulment follows from case law, not from the text of the Awb.
11. **The court may decide the case itself** (8:72 lid 3 onder b). 8:72 then calls the contested decision again with the court's findings, as 7:11 calls the decision it reconsiders. The Awb says that the judgment takes the place of the decision, but not how the court reaches it, so this RFC reuses the mechanism of the objection. The judgment holds the new decision as a decision value.

### Awb 8:70 and 8:72 in rulework

8:70 lists what the judgment can come to. These are its outputs, all on the `UITSPRAAK` item:

| Output | What it is | From |
|---|---|---|
| `beroep_ontvankelijk` | On time (6:9), the contested decision was taken on objection (`taken_on` is 7:11), and the appellant is an interested party | 6:9, 7:1 lid 1, 8:1 |
| `beroep_gegrond` | Null when the appeal is not admissible. Otherwise, whether the court finds that a rule was breached | 8:70 onder c and d, 8:77 lid 2 |
| `uitspraak_strekt_tot` | "niet-ontvankelijkverklaring", "ongegrondverklaring" or "gegrondverklaring van het beroep" | 8:70 onder b to d |
| `geschonden_geoordeelde_rechtsregel` | The rule the judgment names when the appeal is well founded | 8:77 lid 2 |
| `bestreden_besluit_vernietigd` | Whether the contested decision is annulled, which it is when the appeal is well founded | 8:72 lid 1 |
| `rechtsgevolgen_vernietigd` | Whether the legal effects are annulled too, which they are unless the court keeps them | 8:72 lid 2 and lid 3 onder a |
| `opdracht_nieuw_besluit` | Whether the administrative body must take a new decision | 8:72 lid 4 |
| `termijn_nieuw_besluit_einddatum` | The last day the court gives for it, if any | 8:72 lid 4 onder b |
| `uitspraak_in_de_plaats` | The decision the judgment sets in the place of the annulled one, as a decision value | 8:72 lid 3 onder b |

The last five come from 8:72, which 8:70 reads as an input from the same law. 8:70 reads them only when the appeal is well founded, because only then does 8:72 apply ("Indien de bestuursrechter het beroep gegrond verklaart"). Three open terms are declared on 8:72: whether the effects stay, whether the judgment takes the place of the annulled decision (`uitspraak_treedt_in_de_plaats`), and the period for a new decision. The engine therefore asks the court for them only when the appeal is well founded.

```yaml
# Awb 8:70
produces:
  - type: BEROEP
    requests: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#8:70
    parameters: [bestreden_besluit]
  - type: UITSPRAAK
    outputs: [beroep_ontvankelijk, beroep_gegrond, uitspraak_strekt_tot,
              geschonden_geoordeelde_rechtsregel, bestreden_besluit_vernietigd,
              rechtsgevolgen_vernietigd, opdracht_nieuw_besluit,
              termijn_nieuw_besluit_einddatum, uitspraak_in_de_plaats]
```

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Appeal received on the last day of the term | On time. The court judges the grounds |
| Appeal received the day after | Not admissible. The court gives no findings |
| Appeal against the decision on an objection, or against the new decision that it took | Admissible |
| Appeal against a decision on an application, without objection | Not admissible (7:1 lid 1) |
| The appellant is not an interested party | Not admissible (8:1) |
| No rule breached | Unfounded. The decision stands |
| A rule breached, effects not kept, a period set | Well founded. The decision is annulled, and a new decision must follow within the period |
| A rule breached, effects kept | Well founded. The decision is annulled, and no new decision follows |
| The court's finding on the grounds or on the effects is missing | The execution fails with an error. It does not silently read as "unfounded" |

Not modeled:

- the competence of the court (8:70 onder a). 8:6 and 8:7 are not in the corpus, so the court is always competent
- the exceptions of 7:1 lid 1 onder b to g, 6:6, 6:9 lid 2 and 6:11
- partial annulment
- 8:72 lid 4 onder a, lid 5 and lid 6
- the court fee (8:41)
- the appeal to the Raad van State (8:104, 8:105)

### Across the cell boundary

The administrative body and the court each keep a chronicle. "Elke actor houdt eigen feiten bij in een eigen cel", as the position paper has it, and the court is another actor than the administrative body. The law itself sends documents across: the copy of the decision with the beroepschrift (6:5 lid 2), and the copy of the judgment to the parties (8:79 lid 1). The court decides "op de grondslag van ... de overgelegde stukken" (8:69 lid 1). The cell follows that.

| Step | Call | What is recorded |
|---|---|---|
| The administrative body makes a copy of its decision on the objection | `Cell::afschrift(service, sphere, id)` | Nothing. The copy holds the chronolexogram and its decision value, which the cell gets by taking the decision again. It also holds `about`: what the submission behind the decision had received from another cell |
| The court records the appeal | `record_submission(..., Some(Reference::Received(&copy)), ...)` | A `BEROEP` with `received: {from, id}` instead of `refers_to`. Its field `bestreden_besluit` holds the decision value itself, because the court cannot replay the administrative body's decision. Fields the copied decision holds by name come from it, such as `termijn_einddatum` |
| The court gives judgment | `decide(algemene_wet_bestuursrecht, 8:70, ...)` with its findings | An `UITSPRAAK` at the stages UITSPRAAK and TOEZENDING. The findings on 8:72 reach that article through the engine |
| The court makes a copy of the judgment | `Cell::afschrift` | Nothing. `about` names the administrative body's decision, from the appeal's `received` |
| The administrative body records the receipt | `Cell::record_receipt(service, algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#8:79#afschrift_van_de_uitspraak, &copy, refers_to, on)` | An executogram with `refers_to` (its own decision) and `received` (the judgment). Its fields, the parameters the fact holds, come by name from the copied judgment |
| The administrative body decides on the objection again | `Cell::decide_after(..., after: receipt, ...)` | A decision on the same objection, with `after`. The cell allows a decision to be replaced only once. A replacement made by an earlier decision on the objection does not count for that check, so the administrative body can decide again after each annulment |

The cell refuses:

- to make a copy of anything that is not a decision;
- a copy that it made itself, offered back to it as if it came from another cell;
- a receipt for a decision that the copy does not concern, or a receipt dated before the judgment;
- a new decision on a submission without a later chronolexogram that refers to the last decision on it.

The court takes its judgment again from the copy it keeps, so `Cell::explain` works in the court's cell. When a submission has two decisions, taking the second one again finds that decision, not the first.

### A decision that no longer counts

The chronicle keeps an annulled decision. Whoever reads the chronicle decides whether that decision still counts ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)). Here that is a reduction of the administrative body: a rulework that reads the chronicle and gives named answers ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048)).

The reduction reads the fact of Awb 8:79 as one more input (`source: {facts: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#8:79#afschrift_van_de_uitspraak}`). The row of a receipt gives the decision it concerns under `refers_to`, just as every row gives its own id under `id`.

Which decisions no longer count follows from the Awb, and it is the same for every administrative body. So it sits in one reduction of the Awb, which has no holder ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048) §1). The reduction of the administrative body calls it with the article that its decisions rest on. It gets back the decisions under that article that still hold (`geldende_besluiten`). The reduction of the Awb reads the decisions, the decisions on objection and the judgments from the chronicle of the cell that runs the calling reduction. Under Awb 8:72 these no longer count:

- a decision that a judgment annulled with its legal effects (lid 1 and 2), but not one whose effects the court kept (lid 3 onder a);
- the new decision that an annulled decision on objection took in place of a revoked one, because its legal effects go too (lid 2);
- the revocation made by an annulled decision on objection. The objection revives, and the decision it contested counts again as before;
- a decision that the court revoked itself, in the decision on objection that it set in place of the annulled one (lid 3 onder b).

A decision that the court annulled with its legal effects therefore no longer limits what the administrative body decides when it takes up the objection again.

### The court's own decision

8:72 lid 3 onder b lets the court decide that "zijn uitspraak in de plaats treedt van het vernietigde besluit": its judgment takes the place of the annulled decision. The court then takes the decision that the administrative body should have taken. In rulework, 8:72 calls the contested decision again (`source.article: $bestreden_besluit`), as 7:11 calls the decision it reconsiders. 8:70 gives the result as `uitspraak_in_de_plaats`. Lid 4 does not apply in that case, so there is no order for a new decision.

The court's own decision needs three things. The first two are changes to the cell. The third is in the corpus: the fact of Awb 8:79 and the reduction of the Awb.

| Need | What changes |
|---|---|
| The court calls the decision on the objection again, and that decision calls the refusal again | A copy nests decision values. When a field of type `decision` holds an id, `Cell::afschrift` replaces that id in the copy with the value of the decision it names, nested the same way. The chronicle of the administrative body keeps the id ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)), but the receiving cell could not look that id up |
| The court's judgment holds a decision of the kind the administrative body takes | The cell records a decision output as a new decision only when it has the legal character of the deciding article. A `BESCHIKKING` inside an `UITSPRAAK` stays a value of the judgment. A court is not an administrative body (Awb 1:1 lid 2 onder c) and takes no besluit itself |
| The administrative body counts the court's decision | The fact of 8:79, `afschrift_van_de_uitspraak`, holds a decision value: its parameter `uitspraak_in_de_plaats` has type `decision`. The cell already accepts a decision value in a fact. In the row of the receipt that value becomes a row of its own, with the id of the receipt and every output that its article declares. The reduction of the Awb counts the decisions the court set in place under a given article among those that hold. That is the value itself, or, when the court took the decision on objection anew, the new decision inside it (`nieuw_besluit`). The reduction of the administrative body reads them like its own decisions |

The decision value does not hold the terms that the administrative body's reduction fills, so the court has to give a finding on them. The court may supplement the facts (8:69 lid 3). Without that finding the judgment fails; it does not read a fact that nobody gave.

The same goes for a term that a policy of the administrative body fills with an answer from its own cell. The court's cell has no such answer. In that case the policy lets the authority judge the case through an open term of its own ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048) §2), and the court gives that finding.


## Why

### Benefits

- The appeal needs little code. `BEROEP` joins `SUBMISSION_KINDS`, and `UITSPRAAK` joins the legal characters in the editor. Articles 8:70 and 8:72 are rulework.
- The annulment is an outcome of the judgment. Whatever reads the judgment later finds the annulment there.

### Alternatives Considered

- **8:72 as a hook on the judgment, at stage UITSPRAAK.** The first design did this, on the model of 6:7 as a hook on a beschikking. It fails for two reasons. The outputs of a hook are not part of the decision value. And a scenario that runs without stages never reaches the stage.
- **One open term `geschonden_rechtsregel`, null for unfounded.** A required open term cannot be null. An optional one would turn a missing finding into "unfounded".

## Implementation

- Schema v0.8.10 accepts `BEROEP` wherever a kind of submission is allowed (`produces.submission.kind`, the `type` of a list item, a hook's `applies_to.submission`), and `UITSPRAAK` wherever a legal character is allowed.
- The law model and the shared editor code know `BEROEP` as a kind of submission. The editor offers `UITSPRAAK` as a legal character.
- The Awb rulework in the corpus gains the procedure `beroep`. Articles 6:5 and 6:9 also apply to an appeal. The names they share with 6:7 and 6:8 no longer say "bezwaar", for instance `termijn_einddatum` and `bezwaar_of_beroepschrift_tijdig_ingediend`. Articles 8:70 and 8:72 are executable. Articles 7:1, 8:1, 8:77, 8:104 and 8:105 are recorded as text only, without executable logic. The scenarios are in `beroep.feature`.
- Limits:
  - A well-founded judgment may leave the breached rule empty. 8:77 lid 2 says the judgment names it, but nothing checks that.
  - 8:72 assumes that the appeal is well founded. It is meant to be read through 8:70. Run on its own, it does not check this.
  - To make a copy, the cell takes the decision again. A copy therefore costs as much as a replay.
- The cell gains the copy (`Afschrift`), the reference to a received copy (`Reference`, `Received`), the receipt (`Cell::record_receipt`) and deciding again (`Cell::decide_after`). Chronolexograms can hold `received` and `after`. The stages of a decision come from its procedure. The tests in `packages/cel/tests/beroep.rs` play the whole chain with two cells.
- The corpus gains a fictional court cell under `corpus/cells/`, and Awb 8:79 requires the fact `afschrift_van_de_uitspraak`.
- The corpus gains the reduction of the Awb, `reductie_algemene_wet_bestuursrecht`, of no cell (`corpus/cells/reductions/`). Given an article, its article 1 gives the decisions under it that still stand, its article 2 those the court set in place and, together, those that hold (`geldende_besluiten`). Each reduction of an administrative body that counts its decisions calls it.
- A reduction reads receipts as it reads any fact. The row of an executogram also gives `refers_to` (`reading::ROW_REFERS_TO`), so a fact may not have a parameter of that name.
- The court can decide the case itself through the open term `uitspraak_treedt_in_de_plaats` on 8:72. The engine asks for it only when the legal effects do not stay. A copy nests the decision values its fields hold. A decision output of another legal character stays a value. The fact of 8:79 can hold the court's decision as a decision value.
- What is still open about the appeal is listed under [Applications, Decisions and Objections](/concepts/administrative-procedures#open-questions).
