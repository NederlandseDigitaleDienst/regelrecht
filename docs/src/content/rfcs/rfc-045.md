## Context

Leerplichtwet 1969 art. 13a lid 1 lets the head of a school grant leave "op aanvraag", on application. The article says nothing more about that application. The rules for it are in the Algemene wet bestuursrecht (Awb):

- Awb 4:2 says what an application must contain.
- Awb 4:13 says when the decision on it is due.
- Awb 7:11 says how the authority decides on an objection against the decision.

Art. 13a names none of these articles.

The law format could not express this:

- Hooks ([RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007)) select the decisions they apply to by legal character. An application is not a decision, so no hook could apply to it.
- [RFC-008](/rfcs/rfc-008) has an `AANVRAAG` stage. A hook in that stage fires only when a caller runs the decision from that stage, not when someone submits an application.
- No article could take the decision of another article a second time, as an objection requires.

## Decision

The law format records two things: what someone submits, and what the authority decides on it. How a cell records the submission and the decision is in [RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046). A cell is where one organization records its submissions and decisions. [Applications, Decisions and Objections](/concepts/administrative-procedures) shows all the pieces together.

1. **`produces` can be a list.** An article that yields more than one thing gets one item per `type`. The type is either a kind of submission (`AANVRAAG` for an application, `BEZWAAR` for an objection) or a legal character, such as `BESCHIKKING`.

   - A submission lists its `parameters`: what the applicant supplies. Its `requests` names the decision it asks for.
   - A decision lists its `outputs` and its `derived` data.

   Every output belongs to at most one item. An output may belong to no item, but only when a decision in the same article lists its own outputs; such an output is then not part of that decision. The engine computes an output only when something reads it ([RFC-043](/rfcs/rfc-043)).
2. **A hook can apply to a submission** (`applies_to.submission`). `established_by` narrows it to the submission one article establishes. Such a hook takes no `stage` and no `decision_type`. A hook names either a legal character or a submission, never both.
3. **`produces.moment` names the date the law counts from.** It points at the parameter that holds that date, for example the day the application was received, with its `legal_basis`. The engine does not read it. A runtime that records submissions does: it fills that parameter with the day it records the submission.
4. **Each item has its own stage.** The stage named after the submission kind, such as `AANVRAAG`, fires the hooks on the submission and computes no decision output. The other stages compute the decision. When a caller runs an article outside any procedure, every hook fires, as before.
5. **A decision gets its inputs from three places, in this order:**

   1. the submission;
   2. policy that fills an open term (`implements`). An open term is a term the law leaves to someone else to fill in;
   3. the authority's findings (`decided_per_case_by`). A finding is what the authority itself decides on such a term in a single case. The caller passes findings separately from the parameters.

   When a policy fills an open term, the term gets the policy's value, and the engine does not ask the authority for a finding. The engine refuses a finding that no article uses. A policy may use the answer of a cell. When the policy wants the authority to judge a case itself, the policy has to say so ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048) §2).
6. **A decision can be taken again.** The type `decision` holds the value of a decision. `source.article` calls the decision of an article, either by reference or by value. The call reuses the fields and findings of that decision, plus any findings given anew.
7. **An objection is a second submission.** Awb 7:11 establishes the objection. It decides on it by calling the contested decision again.

   - The objection is admissible when it is on time and is not against a decision on an earlier objection (`taken_on`).
   - The objection is founded when an output of the new decision differs from the contested one. A difference in `derived` data does not count.

```yaml
# Leerplichtwet 1969, art. 13a
produces:
  - type: AANVRAAG
    requests: leerplichtwet_1969#13a
    parameters: [bsn_jongere, eerste_verlofdag, laatste_verlofdag]
  - type: BESCHIKKING
    outputs: [verlofdagen, verlof_raakt_eerste_twee_lesweken, verlof_verleend]
```

## Why

### Benefits

- The application exists on its own, as it does in the Awb. A change to Awb 4:2 reaches every application through one new version of the Awb rulework.
- One article holds both the application and the decision, as the text of art. 13a does.
- The runtime does not decide whether an objection is founded. Awb 7:11 calls the contested article again, on the facts of the day on which it decides the objection.
- A runtime that keeps no chronicle can execute the same law.

### Tradeoffs

- Every run of art. 13a also runs Awb 4:2 and 4:13.
- A hook parameter that the caller does not pass must be `required: false`.
- A runtime must run stages to keep the application and the decision apart.

Open questions are listed in [Applications, Decisions and Objections](/concepts/administrative-procedures#open-questions).

### Alternatives Considered

- **The application as the first stage of the decision**, as RFC-008 had it. Its hooks then do not fire when the application comes in, only when the decision runs.
- **The specific law names the Awb** (`includes: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:2`). The legal text does not say this. An author who forgets the line loses the Awb fields without any warning.
- **`legal_character: AANVRAAG`.** An application is not a decision (Awb 1:3).
- **One object with both `submission` and `legal_character`.** The engine then needed a rule of its own to skip the outputs in the AANVRAAG stage.
- **An `id` per item, and `decides_on` on the decision** (PR #1689). The law has no such names. The link also points the wrong way: the application asks for a decision, not the other way round. For the objection, a key on Awb 6:4 with a link from 7:11 would add a second key for what `requests` already says.
- **A separate article for the decision.** The text of art. 13a does not split application and decision.
- **Decision inputs as parameters.** They would become fields that the applicant fills in.
- **A field per parameter saying who supplies it** (`origin`). Who supplies a value is a matter of process, not of law.

## Deviations from earlier RFCs

This RFC departs from three earlier RFCs. Their text stays as it is.

- [RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007): a hook on a legal character also matches a `produces` item with that `type`.
- [RFC-008](/rfcs/rfc-008): the application is an item of its own, not the first stage of the decision. AANVRAAG remains the stage in which its hooks fire.
- [RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022): a decretogram, the recorded decision, is also an item with `type: BESCHIKKING`.

## Implementation

| Schema | Adds |
|---|---|
| v0.8.0 | `produces.submission`, `produces.moment`, `applies_to.submission`, `established_by` |
| v0.8.1 | `produces` as a list |
| v0.8.3 | `BEZWAAR` |
| v0.8.4 | the type `decision`, `source.article`, `source.parameters_from`, `derived` |

The engine adds hooks on a submission to the hooks index, and has `LawExecutionService::execute_stage_at`, `resolve_article_call`, `load_check::reject_ambiguous_produces`. The corpus has Leerplichtwet 1969 art. 13a, the Awb articles above and BABW art. 49, with scenarios.
