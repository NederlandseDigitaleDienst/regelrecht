---
title: "Open Questions in Chronolexography"
description: "The choices in the chronolexography implementation that are still open, and the parts that need investigation or a large change."
---

This page lists what is still open in [chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography), one row per question. The RFCs link here instead of keeping their own lists. When a question is settled, its row goes.

A question is listed when:

- a choice is still to be made;
- it needs investigation first;
- it needs a large refactor or a breaking change to the schema.

Missing functionality that only needs building is not listed. It gets built when a case needs it. A limitation of the proof of concept is not listed either, unless it affects how chronolexography works.

Open questions kept elsewhere:

- the Awb procedure in the law format: [Applications, Decisions and Objections](/concepts/administrative-procedures#open-questions);
- the [open questions of RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022#open-questions), still with its authors: cell roles, security-context binding, update cadence, and whether Blauwe Knop and FSC are one mechanism.

The impact column says how far an answer reaches:

- **Foundational**: the answer decides whether chronolexography works between real organizations and with real registers.
- **High**: the question touches every new case, or it needs a breaking schema change or a large refactor.
- **Medium**: it limits a case, or needs a small change to the engine or the schema.
- **Low**: a local choice, or a matter of documentation.

| Question | Why open | Impact | RFC |
|---|---|---|---|
| `REDUCTIE`, `source.projections`, `source.facts` and `combine: ONLY` exist for the cell, not for the law. Should they leave the law format and move to a profile of their own? Today only `cel.yaml` says which answers a cell gives. A profile would also settle whether picking one record needs its own operations beside `combine: ONLY`, such as taking the latest record (`LATEST`) or the first that matches (`FIND`). | Choice; breaking schema change | High | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| A cell can receive a copy of another cell's decision. It keeps the decision value as it came in. It cannot check that value against the other cell's chronicle, so it trusts the copy, as a court trusts the documents a party submits. Should a receiving cell be able to check a copy at its source? | Choice; investigation | Foundational | [053](/rfcs/rfc-053) |
| A chronolexogram (an entry in the chronicle) has one date, `recorded_on`. The cell uses it as the day of receipt (Awb 4:13, 6:9) and as the day a decision is made known (Awb 6:8). Another cell may record something late, or give it an earlier date. That changes what a replay of a decision sees. Does a chronolexogram need a second date for the moment that counts in law? | Choice; breaking change to the chronicle | Foundational | [046](/rfcs/rfc-046), [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| To explain a decision, the cell takes it again on its original day (a replay). That assumes every source can say what it knew on a given day. A source that keeps only its current state needs a facade that stores each value with its date. How that works for real registers needs investigation. | Investigation | Foundational | [046](/rfcs/rfc-046) |
| Who fixes the name of an answer? A binding asks a cell for an answer by name, and the cell's catalogue has to give the answer under that name. Who sets the name, and how it is versioned, is open. The position paper names Consumer Driven Contracts as a research question here. | Choice; investigation | Medium | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| A reduction is rulework that reads the cell's chronicle and gives a named answer. Some reductions read no chronicle themselves and only ask for another answer of the same cell. Should such a reduction be policy of the authority whose cell keeps it instead? | Choice | Low | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| The engine asks the cell that a term names wherever the policy that binds the term runs. RFC-052 asked it only when another law reached the term through `source.regulation`. Should the engine route by competence everywhere, or only where another law reads the term? | Choice | Low | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| The engine builds the parameters of a binding before it asks a cell. If the binding cannot fill a parameter, the decision fails as soon as there is a cell to ask, even a cell that turns out not to give the answer. Should a missing parameter make the binding silent instead? | Choice | Medium | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| Which instance does the law mean? The law names a category, and the engine asks the cell of that category in the call. The cell does not pass its own identity to the law. So the engine does not find a policy scoped by `gemeente_code`, and cannot check competence by place of residence. See also [#1029](https://github.com/NederlandseDigitaleDienst/regelrecht/issues/1029). | Choice | High | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| A term that reads a fact repeats the list of authorities competent for that fact, and the two lists can drift apart. To derive the term's list from the fact, the engine would need to know which fact a term reads. Should it? | Choice | Low | [051](/rfcs/rfc-051) |
| The cell takes the inputs of a decision only from the deciding article. A finding that the law places in another regulation must therefore sit on the deciding article and be passed on as a parameter. The rulework then departs from the law. | Large refactor | High | [046](/rfcs/rfc-046) |
| When a policy fills an open term, the policy's value counts. So the authority cannot depart from the policy in a single case, although Awb 4:84 allows that. A policy can let the authority judge certain cases itself, with an open term of its own, but only the cases the policy describes. How should the authority record a departure that the policy does not foresee? | Choice | High | [045](/rfcs/rfc-045), [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| The [version history](/reference/schema#version-history) says what each version adds to the schema. It does not say what changed in the engine and the cell at the same time, although a rulework author notices those changes too. Nothing checks that the newest version has a row. Should each version get release notes, split into schema and behavior, with a CI check, filled in back to v0.8.0? | Choice | Low | none |
| A reduction filters rows with a `FOREACH`, once per question. A filter on a source would let a reduction ask only for the rows it needs, for example a `where` on an input, or a lambda passed to a reduction it calls. Since schema v0.8.17 `source.projections` has such a `where`, by the values its roots hold; `source.facts` has none. For a later change. | Choice | Low | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| The Awb does not provide for an applicant to withdraw an application before the decision; only case law recognizes that. Nor does it provide for withdrawing a favorable decision at the holder's request, except for subsidies (Awb 4:48–4:50). Article 6:21 is about withdrawing an objection or appeal, not an application. No effect in the Awb therefore leaves out a decision that was withdrawn on request, such as granted leave that the holder wants moved to other days. We do not fill this in with a rule of our own. | Not in the law; left open on purpose | Medium | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| One question to several cells of a category. The law names a category, such as the college of any municipality, and more than one cell of it may hold the answer. How that works in practice, and which cell is asked, is open. Until then the engine asks one cell and merges no answers. If the call holds more than one cell that the term names, the decision fails. | Choice; investigation | Medium | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| A step of a reduction says which output of a law each property of the state takes, through `input` and `source.regulation`. That reference could move into the law itself, for example as an entry on the `produces` item that points at the article, and the step would disappear from the reduction. The alternative is that the reduction says per `produces` item what it does to the state. Which of the two holds once more cases use a projection is open. | Choice | Medium | |
| A law a step calls may read nothing but what the step passes. The engine checks that on the whole article: one `input` anywhere refuses it, also when the output the step takes does not depend on it. The check should eventually look only at the actions the step uses, that is the output it takes and what that depends on. For now the article as a whole is checked. | Choice | Low | |
| The actions of a step take only a bare reference as their value: an input, a field of the record or a property of the state. Whether a reduction needs operations of its own there is to be found out. Until a case needs one, the rule stays strict, so what a record does stays in the law. | Choice | Low | |
| Should a reduction and a regelwerk be written in the same YAML language? They share the expression language: operations, types, references, `input` with `source`, the engine, the load checks and the trace, and steps now take inputs as articles do. Yet a `REDUCTIE` takes the shape of a law and refuses most of it: no `competent_authority`, hooks, overrides, `produces`, open terms or `implements`, and `text` and `legal_basis` mean nothing there. That suggests a different kind of document. Schema v0.8.14 adds syntax that only a reduction uses, `$state`, `$record` and the `#<KIND>` suffix on a step's record, and v0.8.17 adds `projection.per` and `source.projections`, which makes the question stronger. Since v0.8.17 a `REDUCTIE` comes in two forms only: an aggregate (a projection with `per`, without `execution`) and a read model (an `execution` that reads the aggregates). An RFC that makes the reduction a document type of its own can start from those two. One direction is a shared schema for the building blocks with two roots: a regelwerk with articles, text and a layer, and a reduction with a projection, an execution and the answers it gives. That goes together with renaming `regulatory_layer`, for which a name such as data or data operations is in view. It changes the format and needs an RFC. | Choice | High | |
| The engine plays every aggregate of a kind on every question that reads them (`source.projections`), from the first record of the chronicle. That is fine for the PoC. A real cell with many decisions would need to keep the last state of each aggregate as a snapshot, and then the version of the reduction in force on the calculation date may differ from the one that played it. A snapshot changes how fast a row is read, never what it holds, so this is a question for the engine and the cell. How a cell keeps and invalidates snapshots is open. | Investigation | Medium | |
| The court's cell can decide on an appeal (`BEROEP`), but not on a request for a provisional measure (Awb 8:81): the schema knows `AANVRAAG`, `BEZWAAR` and `BEROEP` as kinds of submission. So the PoC records the receipt of the voorzieningenrechter's ruling at the municipality without playing the ruling in the court's cell, and a test builds that receipt as `Cell::record_receipt` would. Should the schema get a submission kind for a request (verzoek), or should a request for a provisional measure be recorded under an existing kind such as `BEROEP`? | Choice; schema change | Medium | [053](/rfcs/rfc-053) |
| The reduction plays in the version in force on the calculation date. A new version that points a step at another article reads the whole history again in the new way. That is defensible while a reduction only couples outputs to properties, and it belongs with the question of which date and which version apply. | Choice | Low | |
| Article 2, first paragraph, of the Regeling gehandicaptenparkeerkaart issues no card without a medical examination, and article 3, first paragraph, has the GGD or an expert the municipality appoints carry it out. As the law reads, the college asks the GGD for the examination and the GGD then carries it out; Awb 3:2, under which the authority gathers the knowledge it needs about the facts, supports that reading. The PoC does not model the request: the GGD records its advice without one from the municipality, and the advice also proves the examination. A possible next step records the request in the municipality's chronicle, after which the GGD records its advice on that request and refers to it. No choice has been made. | Choice | Low | [051](/rfcs/rfc-051), [052](/rfcs/rfc-052) |
| The engine no longer reads from `decided_per_case_by` who decides. RFC-031 describes the field as the name of the authority that judges the norm in each case. The proposal is that the field only marks that the norm is judged per case. The term's `competent_authority` then says who judges. Without one, the authority that carries out the article judges ([Competent Authority](/concepts/competent-authority#who-gives-a-finding)). This still has to be aligned with RFC-031. | Choice | Low | [031](/rfcs/rfc-031), [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| From the law and the policy, the engine derives which open terms a decision may need and who answers each one (`decision_terms`). The cell adds the parameters from the application and the date of the decision. The process decides when and how it asks the authority. Should the engine also say which of a policy's own open terms a case needs, without running the decision? | Choice | Medium | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| A reduction, or the policy it calls, can pick one record with `combine: ONLY`. It fails when two records qualify. A correction recorded on the same day then blocks the decision, and the chronicle cannot undo it, because entries are never removed. Should a later record replace an earlier one? A reduction with a `projection` (schema v0.8.14, experimental) can already let a later record set a property again, so the last one counts, and a fact that says an earlier one was wrong from the start can revoke it (`packages/cel/bdd/features/corrections.feature`); `ONLY` itself is unchanged. | Choice | Foundational | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| The fictitious parking ordinance of Wilgendaal lets the college revoke a business parking permit, at the holder's request or when the company no longer works in the area (article 5, after article 6 of the Delft model). Revocation is a decision the authority takes without an application. Since schema v0.8.17 the cell can take one (`Cell::decide_ex_officio`), referring to the decision it affects, but the Wilgendaal case does not use it yet. The Awb of the PoC revokes only a decision reversed on objection (7:11, herroeping) and one annulled by the court (8:72), and the Awb has no general article on revoking a decision, so a revoked permit would still count. The same holds for a company that moves out of an area: its permits count until they expire. Where does it belong that a revoked decision no longer holds: in the ordinance that grants the power, read by the municipality's own reduction, or in a general rule beside the Awb? The synthetic law of the cell's BDD suite tries the first: its withdrawal is a decision taken ex officio that declares `revokes`. | Choice | High | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048), [050](/rfcs/rfc-050) |
| The Kamer van Koophandel records its registrations as a fact of a fictitious policy of its own, not of the Handelsregisterwet 2007. That law is not in the regelrecht corpus: the NAPP source holds it with executable articles of that case, and a version under the same `$id` here would shadow the source's. The number of people working in a company is not in the law either; an order in council may add it to the register (article 17, first paragraph, under a), and that order, the Handelsregisterbesluit 2008, is not in the corpus. Should the Handelsregisterwet move into the central corpus, with the facts the Chamber records as `requires` on its own articles? | Choice; investigation | Medium | [051](/rfcs/rfc-051) |
| Only policy may bind an open term to a cell's answer (`source.reduction` in `UITVOERINGSBELEID`). The implementing decision of Delft that the Wilgendaal case follows lays down further rules under the ordinance, so as a legal instrument it is not policy. The schema has no layer for further rules of the college, so the Wilgendaal version is a `GEMEENTELIJKE_VERORDENING`, and a separate fictitious policy of the college binds its terms to the answers of the cells. The implementing decision carries a `gemeente_code`, as the ordinance does; the policy carries none. A cell passes no code to the engine, so the engine lets a rulework of the same municipality fill the terms of the ordinance whatever the scope. Should further rules get a layer of their own, and should the scope come from the cell? | Choice; schema change | Medium | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| The Delft model also caps the number of permits per area across all companies, with a waiting list and priorities, and lots among equal applications (articles 5 and 6 of its implementing decision). The Wilgendaal case leaves that out and caps permits per company only. A cap across applicants makes one decision depend on the applications of others, and on their order, which a replay has to reproduce; drawing lots does not replay at all. How a cell decides on a cap shared by several applicants is open. | Choice; investigation | Medium | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| A decision can rest on a finding of a person instead of a calculation. The fictitious parking ordinance of Wilgendaal has a hardship clause (article 6, after article 13 of the Parkeerverordening Deventer 2026). Under it, the college decides per case whether the maximum leads to unacceptable consequences. The engine asks for that finding only for a company at the maximum. The cell records the finding among the fields of the decision, as a given beside what the Kamer van Koophandel answered. The chronicle does not record why the college decided so. The grounds of the objection stay in the objection, and the weighing itself is recorded nowhere. Yet Awb 3:46, and on objection 7:12, requires the decision to rest on sound reasons. A replay of the decision takes the recorded finding. Without it the engine refuses, because the term is required and nothing fills it. Should the chronicle keep the reasons with the finding? And should a replay under a changed rule reuse the finding or ask for it again? | Choice; investigation | High | [046](/rfcs/rfc-046), [050](/rfcs/rfc-050) |
| Could the college depart from the maximum without a hardship clause? Without one, the refusal on the maximum is a bound power in a regulation below formal law. ABRvS 2 February 2022, ECLI:NL:RVS:2022:285 (Harderwijk), gives the test of proportionality under Awb 3:4, second paragraph, for decisions with discretion. It leaves bound powers open (considerations 7.1 and 7.2), so the answer does not follow from it. If the college can, a cell needs a way to record a departure from a bound power that no open term foresees, as the row on Awb 4:84 asks for a policy. | Choice; investigation | Medium | [045](/rfcs/rfc-045), [050](/rfcs/rfc-050) |
| Scenarios get the data a law reads from outside through the `DataSourceRegistry`. The caller registers rows, and the engine finds a value by the name of the input and the parameters of the article. The demo builds those rows from `corpus/demo/bindings.yaml`, which also says per input whether a missing row means unknown, null or zero ([RFC-036](/rfcs/rfc-036)). Should a scenario build a chronicle instead, with a fixture cell that gives the answers the test needs? The engine would then fetch outside data in one way only, by asking a cell, and the registry and the bindings could go. Today a plain law input cannot ask a cell at all: only policy binds a term to an answer. Which cell answers such an input, and under what name, is open; names of inputs clash between laws. A fixture cell can also answer what no real cell gives. Its answers would have to be checked against the catalogue in `cel.yaml` of the cell it stands in for, and each real cell still needs scenarios that run through its chronicle and its reduction. | Choice; large refactor | High | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| A `source` on an input is an object of ten optional keys, and the key that is present says what kind of source it is. A series of `dependencies` rules in the schema forbids the combinations that make no sense; the `Source` struct of the law model holds only `Option`s. Five kinds came with chronolexography: `decisions`, `article`, `facts`, `reduction` and `projections`; since schema v0.8.17 `projections` replaces `decisions`. Each had to fit beside the keys already there, which is where most of the exclusion rules come from. Two forms do not say what they are. `source: {}` is a value the caller supplies (see the row above), and `{output: x}` without `regulation` is an output of another article of the same law. Should `source` get a required kind, as in `source: {kind: reduction, ref: aantal_geldige_bedrijfsparkeervergunningen, parameters: {kvk_nummer: $kvk_nummer}}`? The kinds would be `data`, `regulation` (without `ref` for the law itself), `article`, `reduction`, `projections` and `facts`. In the law model a tagged enum would then make an invalid combination impossible to write. Whether `projections` and `facts` become one kind is open as well: both serve only a `REDUCTIE` and both read the chronicle. The change breaks every rulework, about 1,090 sources in some 170 files in this repository, and corpus repositories elsewhere have to migrate with it. | Choice; breaking schema change | High | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| Awb 4:2, first paragraph, under c, requires an application to indicate the decision it asks for. The `requests` on the article that establishes the application names that decision, yet the PoC still asks for it as a parameter of 4:2. The starting point is that the engine fills the parameter from `requests` itself. Where no article names `requests`, as for an application made directly under the Awb, the applicant fills it in. How the Awb rulework links the parameter to `requests` is open. First, a new element like `produces.moment` names the parameter; the cell then fills it, not the engine. Second, the parameter becomes an input whose `source` points to that `requests`. The engine resolves that source, and the input cannot fall back to what the applicant enters. Third, a context variable such as `referencedate` adds a name to the law format that no rulework declares. | Choice; schema change | Medium | [045](/rfcs/rfc-045) |
| A decision taken ex officio (`Cell::decide_ex_officio`) rests on no submission. Replaying a decision starts from the submission it was taken on, so the cell cannot explain such a decision or give its value in a copy, and an objection against it cannot reach the decision value. Should a replay take the fields the decision holds as supplied, as the cell did when it took the decision? | Choice | Medium | [046](/rfcs/rfc-046), [053](/rfcs/rfc-053) |
| When the court decides the case itself (Awb 8:72, third paragraph, under b), its ruling holds the decision it puts in place. The receiving cell records the receipt, not that decision. In the PoC a step of the reduction reads the decision in place from the receipt, so leave the court grants counts as granted leave, and a card it grants makes the cell silent on a valid card, also after that card has expired, so from then on the college judges. What that decision does with another, such as revoking a grant of the authority, is an effect on a record the receipt does not refer to, so that part is not expressed. Should the receiving cell record the decision in place as a record of its own, with its effects, or should a receipt be able to revoke more than the record it refers to? | Choice | Medium | [053](/rfcs/rfc-053) |
| A suspension by the voorzieningenrechter (Awb 8:81) is about one decision. Once a decision on an objection puts a new permit in the place of the contested decision, a suspension touches the permit only if it is about that decision on the objection. A step reads the fields, the outputs and `refers_to` of its record, not its `id`, so the aggregate of a permit cannot hold which decision on the objection took the new one. The PoC keeps the decision the last ruling of the voorzieningenrechter in the aggregate is about (`voorziening_op_besluit`). A later ruling about the revoked decision, such as a rejection received after the permit on objection was suspended, then lifts that suspension in the PoC. Should a step read the `id` of its record, or should a suspension be matched another way? | Choice | Low | [053](/rfcs/rfc-053) |
| A court may annul a decision in part (Awb 8:72, first paragraph). `revokes` takes a whole record. A partial annulment is then a revocation of the whole decision and a record of the part that stays, such as the new decision the authority takes on the court's order. The language has no partial revocation, and nothing yet asks for one. | Choice | Low | [053](/rfcs/rfc-053) |
| A step calls a rulework with `source.regulation`, and that rulework may not be the one whose article asked the cell for the answer: the engine is still resolving it, and refuses. The PoC therefore keeps what one decision means in a policy of its own beside the policy that binds the term. Should the engine allow it, or is the separate rulework the better place anyway? | Choice | Low | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| `Cell::explain_answer` replays a decision and runs the reduction again, while the trace of the decision now holds the same run under the answer. The PoC builds its account of each answer on it, by how many times the decision asked the cell that answer, which the nested trace does not number. Should it go, and should the PoC read the nested run instead? | Choice | Low | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| A step reads `$record.<field>` only as the law of the record's article declares it. A decision holds every parameter of its article, so a step reads a date the article declares itself, such as `besluit_datum` on article 2 of the parking regulation of Wilgendaal. A field that only a hook or a stage of the procedure declares is in the record as well, but a step cannot read it. Should the loader take the procedure into account, as the cell does when it derives what a decision holds? | Choice | Low | none |
| When a reduction loads, the loader checks its steps against the newest version it holds of each law they name, and the play checks them against the versions in force on the calculation date. A step calls the law in the version in force on the day of the record, so one version of a reduction may meet several versions of a law. After a version that renumbers the article or adds a required parameter that the output reads, the loader and the play refuse the reduction, even where every record predates that version. The loader does so as soon as that version is loaded, before it is in force, so whether the reduction loads depends on whether it arrives before or after that version. Should the loader check each version of the law in force while the reduction version is? | Choice | Medium | none |
| A step reads only an output that reads no input and no open term of its article. An override or a hook from another regulation can still change that output when the step calls it. Should the loader refuse a step whose output another regulation overrides? | Investigation | Low | none |

## Next steps

In order of impact, as of 27 October 2026. A question that waits for a case to need it comes last.

1. Time and corrections in the chronicle: a second date for the moment that counts in law, and whether a later record replaces an earlier one. One design covers both, since both decide what a replay sees. It needs investigation first and then an RFC.
2. A source that keeps only its current state. Try a facade on one real register before more cases assume one.
3. Checking a received copy at its source.
4. A policy that fills an open term is final. `policy_first` and the fallback to a finding are removed; a departure in a single case is the Awb 4:84 question. Working out what is asked of whom moves out of the cell. In progress; it changes [RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048) §2.
5. Inputs that belong in a regulation other than the deciding article. This widens what a cell can decide, and every case after the PoC needs it.

After that: release notes for the schema, and building out the NAPP case. The Awb questions on 4:1 with 4:5 and on the motivation requirements belong there too ([open questions of the Awb procedure](/concepts/administrative-procedures#open-questions)). Later: one question to several cells of a category, and a profile of its own for what serves a cell.
