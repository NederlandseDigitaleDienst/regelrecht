---
title: "Open Points in Chronolexography"
description: "The choices in the chronolexography implementation that are still open, and the parts that need investigation or a large change."
---

What is open in [chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography), one row per point. RFCs link here instead of keeping their own list, and a closed point loses its row.

A point is listed when a choice is still to be made, when it needs investigation first, or when it needs a large refactor or a breaking change to the schema. Missing functionality that only needs building is not listed; it gets built when a case needs it. Neither is a limitation of the proof of concept that does not affect how chronolexography works.

Elsewhere:

- the Awb procedure in the law format: [Applications, Decisions and Objections](/concepts/administrative-procedures#open-points);
- the [open questions of RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022#open-questions), still with its authors: cell roles, security-context binding, update cadence, and whether Blauwe Knop and FSC are one mechanism.

| Point | Why open | RFC |
|---|---|---|
| Should `REDUCTIE`, `source.decisions`, `source.facts` and `combine: ONLY` leave the law format for a profile of their own? They serve a cell, not the law. Today only `cel.yaml` says which answers a cell gives. A profile would also decide whether picking a record needs operations of its own, such as the latest one or the first that matches (`LATEST`, `FIND`), beside `combine: ONLY`. | Choice; breaking schema change | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| A cell that receives a copy of another cell's decision keeps the decision value as it came in. It cannot check that value against the other cell's chronicle, and trusts it as a court trusts the documents a party submits. Should a receiving cell be able to check a copy at its source? | Choice; investigation | [053](/rfcs/rfc-053) |
| A chronolexogram has one date, `recorded_on`. The cell takes it as the day of receipt (Awb 4:13, 6:9) and as the day a decision is made known (Awb 6:8). A record another cell makes late, or dates back, changes what a replay sees. Does a chronolexogram need a second date, for the moment that counts in law? | Choice; breaking change to the chronicle | [046](/rfcs/rfc-046), [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| A replay assumes every source knows what it knew on a given day. A source that holds only its current state needs a facade that stores each value with its moment. How that works for real registers needs investigation. | Investigation | [046](/rfcs/rfc-046) |
| Who fixes the name of an answer? For synthesis several cells give one answer under one name, and a binding asks for that name. Who sets it, and how it is versioned, is open; the position paper names Consumer Driven Contracts as a research question. | Choice; investigation | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| A reduction may read no chronicle itself and only ask, inside it, for another answer of its cell. Should such a reduction be policy of the authority whose cell keeps it instead? | Choice | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| The engine asks the cells a term names wherever a binding runs, where RFC-052 asked them only for a term reached through `source.regulation`. Should competence route there too, or only where another law reads the term? | Choice | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| The engine builds a binding's parameters before it asks. A parameter the binding cannot fill fails the decision as soon as there is a cell to ask, even one that turns out not to give the answer. Should a missing parameter make the binding silent instead? | Choice | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| Which instance does the law mean? It names a category, and the engine asks every cell of that category. The cell does not pass its identity to the law, so a policy scoped by `gemeente_code` is not found and competence by place of residence cannot be checked. See also [#1029](https://github.com/NederlandseDigitaleDienst/regelrecht/issues/1029). | Choice | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| A term that reads a fact names the fact's competent authorities a second time, and the two lists can drift apart. Deriving the term's list from the fact needs the engine to know which fact a term reads. Should it? | Choice | [051](/rfcs/rfc-051) |
| The cell takes a decision's inputs only from the deciding article. A judgment that belongs in another regulation must sit on the deciding article and be passed on as a parameter, so the rulework departs from the law. | Large refactor | [046](/rfcs/rfc-046) |
| When a policy fills an open term, the policy's value counts. So the authority cannot depart from the policy in a single case, although Awb 4:84 allows that. A policy can let the authority judge certain cases itself, with an open term of its own, but only the cases the policy describes. How should the authority record a departure that the policy does not foresee? | Choice | [045](/rfcs/rfc-045), [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| The engine sees a cell as a black box, so `Cell::explain_answer` replays the decision and runs the reduction again, with tracing. If an answerer could return a trace with its answer, the engine would nest it under the answer node. The whole chain (law, policy binding, reduction, the decisions it counted) would then be one trace, and `explain_answer` could go. | Choice; small engine API change | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| The [version history](/reference/schema#version-history) says what each version adds to the schema, but not what changed with it in the engine and the cell, which a rulework author also notices. Nothing checks that the highest version has a row. Should each version get release notes split into schema and behavior, with a CI check, filled in back to v0.8.0? | Choice | none |
| A reduction filters rows with a `FOREACH` per question. A filter on a source (a `where` on an input, or a lambda passed to a reduction it calls) would let a reduction ask for the rows it needs, and the reduction of the Awb would not have to return every row that holds. For a later change. | Choice | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| The Awb does not provide for an applicant to withdraw an application before the decision; only case law recognizes that. Nor does it provide for withdrawing a favorable decision at the holder's request, except for subsidies (Awb 4:48–4:50). Article 6:21 is about withdrawing an objection or appeal, not an application. The reduction of the Awb therefore cannot leave out a decision that was withdrawn on request, such as granted leave that the holder wants moved to other days. We do not fill this in with a rule of our own. | Not in the law; left open on purpose | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| One open term is asked of several cells, the cells of the sphere: every GGD that examines, for example, or every municipality that issued a card. Each cell gives its own answer, and the engine merges the answers with `combine`. Whether that works in practice is open, because all those cells would have to be integrated with each other. An alternative is to find the one suitable cell of a category that answers the question. The glossary and [RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048) now call this mechanism synthesis. Next step: remove the mechanism until it is clear how one question to several cells would work in practice. A pull request of its own does that and corrects the glossary and RFC-048 with it. | Choice; investigation | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048), [052](/rfcs/rfc-052) |
| A reduction is no regulation, yet the reduction of the Awb holds rules that come from the Awb: a decision revoked on objection (Awb 7:11) or annulled by the court (Awb 8:72) no longer holds. That content belongs in the law. The Awb never says in so many words that a decision no longer holds; it follows from revocation (7:11, second paragraph) and annulment (8:72, first paragraph), so there is no article to ask it of directly. Three directions are open. The Awb could give the judgement per decision, as a derived output of 7:11 and 8:72 without `legal_basis`; the reduction would then only collect decisions from the chronicle and ask the Awb for each one whether it still holds, so the law judges and the reduction fetches. An open term "does this decision still hold?" could be filled by a reduction, which leaves the Awb logic in the reduction. Or the reduction stays as it is and the departure is recorded. No choice has been made; the first direction keeps law and reduction apart most cleanly. | Choice | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048), [053](/rfcs/rfc-053) |
| Article 2, first paragraph, of the Regeling gehandicaptenparkeerkaart issues no card without a medical examination, and article 3, first paragraph, has the GGD or an expert the municipality appoints carry it out. As the law reads, the college asks the GGD for the examination and the GGD then carries it out; Awb 3:2, under which the authority gathers the knowledge it needs about the facts, supports that reading. The PoC does not model the request: the GGD records the examination and the advice without one from the municipality. A possible next step records the request in the municipality's chronicle, after which the GGD records the examination on that request and refers to it. No choice has been made. | Choice | [051](/rfcs/rfc-051), [052](/rfcs/rfc-052) |
| The engine no longer reads from `decided_per_case_by` who decides. RFC-031 describes the field as the name of the authority that judges the norm in each case. The proposal is that the field only marks that the norm is judged per case. Who judges then follows from `competent_authority` on the term, or else from the authority that carries out the article ([Competent Authority](/concepts/competent-authority#who-gives-a-finding)). This still has to be aligned with RFC-031. | Choice | [031](/rfcs/rfc-031), [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| The engine derives from law and policy which open terms a decision may need, and who answers each one (`decision_terms`). The cell adds the parameters from the application and the date of the decision. The process itself decides when and how it asks the authority. Should the engine also say which of a policy's own open terms a case needs, without running the decision? | Choice | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| A reduction, or the policy it calls, that picks one record with `combine: ONLY` fails when two records qualify. A correction recorded on the same day then blocks the decision, and the chronicle cannot undo it. Should a later record replace an earlier one? | Choice | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| The fictitious parking ordinance of Wilgendaal lets the college revoke a business parking permit, at the holder's request or when the company no longer works in the area (article 5, after article 6 of the Delft model). Revocation is a decision the authority takes without an application, and the cell takes decisions only on an application or an objection. The reduction of the Awb reads only a decision reversed on objection (7:11, herroeping) and one annulled by the court (8:72), and the Awb has no general article on revoking a decision, so a revoked permit would still count. The same holds for a company that moves out of an area: its permits count until they expire. Where does it belong that a revoked decision no longer holds: in the ordinance that grants the power, read by the municipality's own reduction, or in a general rule beside the Awb? | Choice | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048), [050](/rfcs/rfc-050) |
| The Kamer van Koophandel records its registrations as a fact of a fictitious policy of its own, not of the Handelsregisterwet 2007. That law is not in the regelrecht corpus: the NAPP source holds it with executable articles of that case, and a version under the same `$id` here would shadow the source's. The number of people working in a company is not in the law either; an order in council may add it to the register (article 17, first paragraph, under a), and that order, the Handelsregisterbesluit 2008, is not in the corpus. Should the Handelsregisterwet move into the central corpus, with the facts the Chamber records as `requires` on its own articles? | Choice; investigation | [051](/rfcs/rfc-051) |
| Only policy may bind an open term to a cell's answer (`source.reduction` in `UITVOERINGSBELEID`). The implementing decision of Delft that the Wilgendaal case follows lays down further rules under the ordinance, so as a legal instrument it is not policy. The Wilgendaal version carries the layer `UITVOERINGSBELEID` so that it can bind. Should a regulation that lays down further rules be able to bind a term as well? | Choice; schema change | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| The Delft model also caps the number of permits per area across all companies, with a waiting list and priorities, and lots among equal applications (articles 5 and 6 of its implementing decision). The Wilgendaal case leaves that out and caps permits per company only. A cap across applicants makes one decision depend on the applications of others, and on their order, which a replay has to reproduce; drawing lots does not replay at all. How a cell decides on a cap shared by several applicants is open. | Choice; investigation | [048](/rfcs/rfc-048) |
| A decision can rest on a judgement of a person instead of a calculation. Under the hardship clause of the fictitious parking ordinance of Wilgendaal (article 6, after article 13 of the Parkeerverordening Deventer 2026), the college judges per case whether the maximum leads to unacceptable consequences, and the engine asks it only for a company at the maximum. The cell records the judgement among the fields of the decision, as a given beside what the Kamer van Koophandel answered. The chronicle does not record why the college judged so. The grounds of the objection stay in the objection, and the weighing itself is recorded nowhere. Awb 3:46, and on objection 7:12, does require the decision to rest on sound reasons. A replay of the decision takes the recorded judgement. Without it the engine refuses, because the term is required and nothing fills it. Should the chronicle keep the reasons with the judgement, and should a replay under a changed rule reuse the judgement or ask it again? | Choice; investigation | [046](/rfcs/rfc-046), [050](/rfcs/rfc-050) |
| Could the college depart from the maximum without a hardship clause? Without one, the refusal on the maximum is a bound power in a regulation below formal law. ABRvS 2 February 2022, ECLI:NL:RVS:2022:285 (Harderwijk), gives the test of proportionality under Awb 3:4, second paragraph, for decisions with discretion. It leaves bound powers open (considerations 7.1 and 7.2), so the answer does not follow from it. The fictitious ordinance of Wilgendaal has a clause, so the PoC does not need the answer. | Investigation | [050](/rfcs/rfc-050) |

## Next steps

In order of priority, as of 24 October 2026:

1. A policy that fills an open term is final. `policy_first` and the fallback to a finding go, so one rule decides where a term's value comes from; a departure in a single case is the Awb 4:84 point. Working out what is asked of whom moves out of the cell. In progress; it changes [RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048) §2.
2. One trace for a decision and the cell's answer (`Cell::explain_answer`, in the table above).
3. Release notes for the schema, beside the [version history](/reference/schema#version-history).
4. Awb 4:1 as the start of an application, with 4:5 ([open points of the Awb procedure](/concepts/administrative-procedures#open-points)).
5. The motivation requirements of Awb 3:46 to 3:50, and 3:45 ([open points of the Awb procedure](/concepts/administrative-procedures#open-points)), together with the Awb 4:84 point.
6. Building out the NAPP case.

Later: several instances of one category, and a profile of its own for what serves a cell.
