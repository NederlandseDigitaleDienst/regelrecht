These notes are for someone who writes or maintains a rulework and wants to move its `$schema` to a newer version. Each note says what the version changes for that person and whether a rulework version that validated under the previous schema still validates. When it does not, the note says what to edit. Each note shows its construct in an example.

The [version history](/reference/schema#version-history) on the schema reference stays the record of which version introduced which construct, and it is the table RFCs cite. Each row from v0.8.0 on links to its note here. Where the row records what a version introduced, the note says what an author has to do about it.

From v0.8.0 on, some versions remove a construct or tighten a rule, and the engine in this repository handles that in two ways. For the removals in v0.8.7, v0.8.9, v0.8.12 and v0.8.13 it runs every version on one code path. It refuses the removed construct when it loads the rulework, whatever its `$schema` says, and names the replacement where there is one. Such a rulework still validates against the older schema, but has to be edited before the engine will run it. From v0.8.15 on, the engine applies a new rule only to a rulework that declares the new version, with one exception in [v0.8.17](#v0817). A rulework on an older `$schema` keeps loading as it did, and the edit is due when its `$schema` moves. The one change neither the schema nor the engine catches at load is the rename in [v0.8.12](#v0812).

The notes start at v0.8.0. Versions before it have no note; for those, the version history is all there is.

## Where the examples come from

No example on this page is typed in. Each one is a marked excerpt of a fixture in `packages/engine/tests/conformance/`, read when the site is built. `just conformance` checks every fixture in CI: a fixture under `valid/` must pass the schema and survive a round trip through the Rust model without losing a field, and a fixture under `invalid/` must be refused by the schema version its `$schema` names. A positive example here therefore cannot show something the schema does not accept, and a shape to change comes from a fixture the schema refuses. Each such fixture holds that one construct and otherwise valid metadata.

Each fixture under `valid/` that these notes draw on declares the newest schema that still accepts its construct, and the caption under an example names that version. Most declare v0.8.17. The ones whose construct v0.8.17 removed stay on v0.8.16. A positive example can therefore come from a later version than the note it sits in, and where a later version changed the construct, the note says so.

## v0.8.17

A projection can play one aggregate per record, which other reductions read as rows, and the constructs that made a projection depend on the question are gone. Experimental, without an RFC so far.

Not additive. A rulework version that declares v0.8.16 or earlier keeps loading as it did, `key`, question parameters and `source.decisions` included. Moving a reduction to v0.8.17 takes up to three edits:

- A projection gets `per` in place of `key` and loses its `execution`. The question it answered, about one person or about the whole chronicle, moves to a reduction that reads the aggregates with `source.projections`, and with `where` for one person.
- A step passes only `$record.<field>` and `$state.<property>`, never a parameter of the question (`$<name>`). A step that compared a date of the record with the day of the question now puts the record's date in the state. The reduction that reads the rows passes them, each with its record's date, and the day of the question to an article of the law or policy, which compares them.
- A reduction that read `source.decisions` reads the aggregates of those decisions with `source.projections` instead.

A law that moves to v0.8.17 needs no edit. From then on, the cell refuses a decision under that law that revokes a record its `refers_to` does not reach. A reduction on v0.8.17 with `per` also applies that rule to every decision it plays, whatever version the decision's law declares. The rule can therefore reach a law that was never edited.

`projection.per` names the article the root record rests on, as `<regulation>#<article>`, with `#<KIND>` where the article gives more than one kind of record. An aggregate is the root and the records that refer to it through `refers_to`, directly or through each other. Another record of the root's kind that refers in is an aggregate of its own, and counts in the first one for its effects only. A projection with `per` answers no question itself and has no `execution`. Its steps may read `$record.refers_to`, the id of the record the current record refers to, or null.

`refers_to` names, on every record, the record that record is about. ## v0.8.16

A step of a projection is held to three stricter rules. The article the step is on must give a record. `$record` reads only the fields of the kind of record the step is about. And the step passes every required parameter that the output it takes reads, directly or through another output of the same article. The loader checks the steps when the reduction loads, against the newest version it holds of each law they name, and the engine checks them again when it plays the projection. One rule is looser in every version: a step may take an output of an article that has an `input` or an open term, as long as that output reads neither.

Not additive, although the schema changed only in its descriptions. A reduction on v0.8.15 or earlier still loads under the old rules and is checked only when it plays. A v0.8.15 version that breaks one of the new rules has to be fixed before it can declare v0.8.16.

## v0.8.15

A fact that an article requires may have `effects`, as a decision has them. Its only effect is `revokes: refers_to`, so a fact revokes the record it refers to. `when` names a boolean parameter the fact holds and makes the effect conditional on it. The receipt of a copy of a ruling that annuls a decision (Awb 8:72 lid 1, 8:79) revokes that decision this way:

<FixtureExcerpt fixture="valid/v0_8_15_record_effects.yaml" region="fact-effects" />

An effect of a decision may now name any earlier record, a fact included. `revokes` means the record does not count, from the start (ex tunc). A change from a later day on, such as a suspension (Awb 8:81) or a withdrawal from a date, is no effect. It is a step of the reduction that puts the dates in its state.

The parameter a decision's effect revokes must be a string or a decision, which counts by its id, and may not be nullable:

<FixtureExcerpt fixture="valid/v0_8_15_record_effects.yaml" region="revokes-by-id" />

Not additive, although the schema of v0.8.15 accepts every document that v0.8.14 accepts. The version history counts the version as additive for that reason, but the loader is stricter, as it is for v0.8.16. A version on v0.8.14 still loads. Fix a v0.8.14 version before it declares v0.8.15 if a decision effect names a parameter that is not a string or a decision. The same holds if that parameter may be null.

## v0.8.14

Two additions, both experimental and without an RFC so far. A decision can say what it does with another decision, and a reduction can play its cell's chronicle forward and answer from the state it ends in.

Additive: a rulework version valid under v0.8.13 is valid under v0.8.14 unchanged.

A decision in the list form of `produces` names its `effects`. With `revokes`, the decision revokes the decision held by the parameter it names, as a decision on an objection revokes the contested one (Awb 7:11 lid 2). `when` makes the effect conditional on a boolean output of the same decision, so the decision on the objection revokes only when the objection is well-founded:

<FixtureExcerpt fixture="valid/v0_8_14_decision_effects.yaml" region="revokes-when" />

A decision taken in the place of another revokes it without a condition, and the new decision itself counts:

<FixtureExcerpt fixture="valid/v0_8_14_decision_effects.yaml" region="in-place" />

A `REDUCTIE` may have a `projection`. Its `state` lists named properties with a type, and each starts at the start value of its type: false for a boolean, 0 for a number or an amount, null for a string or a date. `steps` has one entry per article a record rests on. A step takes outputs of a law the way an article does, with an `input` whose `source` is `regulation`. Its `actions` give properties their values. Each value is a bare reference to an input, a field of the record (`$record.<field>`) or the state before it (`$state.<property>`). The step computes nothing itself; what a record does stays in the law it calls. That law runs in the version in force on the day of the record, and may read no `input` and no open term. `record` may name the kind of record in a third segment, such as `#BESCHIKKING`, and has to when the article gives more than one kind. With `key`, only the records of the person asked about are played. The reduction's `execution` answers from the last state.

Which records count follows from the effects: a record counts unless a later record that itself counts revokes it, and only the records that count change the state.

<FixtureExcerpt fixture="valid/v0_8_14_projection.yaml" region="projection" />

A step may also pass a parameter of the question to the law it calls, such as the day the question is about. The answer then depends on that parameter, as it does on the key. This step leaves out the third segment, which is allowed because the article gives one kind of record:

<FixtureExcerpt fixture="valid/v0_8_14_projection_question.yaml" region="question-parameter" />

[v0.8.17](#v0817) removed question parameters and `key`, so this example and the projection above declare v0.8.16.

The engine plays a projection only where a chronicle is played: when a cell gives the reduction's answer to a policy through `source.reduction`, or through `evaluate_projection` in the Rust engine. Any other way of asking for the article (directly, from another rulework, at a stage, or with a `state` of the caller's) makes the engine refuse it.

## v0.8.13

`combine` is gone from open terms (where v0.8.8 put it) and from a `source` beside `reduction` (where v0.8.9 put it). There it told the engine how to merge the answers of several cells. The `combine` of a `FOREACH` is a different field and stays. The engine now asks one cell and merges nothing; when the call that a term names has more than one cell, the input fails at run time.

Not additive. A rulework version that uses `combine` in neither place validates under v0.8.13 unchanged. A version that uses it in either place no longer validates; remove the field. A term may still list several competent authorities. What fails, at run time, is a call in which more than one of them has a cell. How one question would reach several cells of a category is an [open question](/concepts/chronolexography/open-questions), so there is no replacement construct yet.

## v0.8.12

Two changes, both about reductions. The article a decision value was taken under is now called `basis`, where it was `grondslag`: in the decision value, in the rows of `source.decisions` and `source.facts`, and in `$besluit.basis`. And a `REDUCTIE` no longer names a `competent_authority`; the directory it sits in says whose it is.

Not additive. A reduction with a root `competent_authority` is refused: delete the field and put the version under its cell, in `corpus/cells/<cell>/reductions/`, or in `corpus/cells/reductions/` if it belongs to no cell. The rename is one the schema cannot see, because `basis` is the name of a value at run time and not a key in the document. An action that reads `$besluit.grondslag` still validates and still loads, but the decision value it reads has no field of that name any more. Change `.grondslag` to `.basis` wherever an action reads it, such as `$besluit.grondslag`. That covers a decision value and the rows of `source.decisions` and `source.facts`. A plain search for the word also finds parameters and outputs that merely contain it.

## v0.8.11

`source.decisions` may take the article to search under from a parameter, as a `$`-reference, instead of naming it. ## v0.8.9

A reduction stops filling open terms. It becomes a named answer of its cell, and the policy of the authority that decides (`regulatory_layer: UITVOERINGSBELEID`) binds an open term to that answer, with `implements` and an input whose `source` is `reduction`. The policy names its holder in a root `competent_authority`.

Not additive. A `REDUCTIE` with `implements`, which v0.8.5 allowed, is refused. Remove `implements` from the reduction, let its cell give it as an answer under the name the policy will ask for, and move the `implements` to an article of the policy that takes the answer through `source.reduction`.

## v0.8.8

An open term and a required fact may say who is competent for them, with their own `competent_authority`: one named authority or a list. On the term it names who may answer it, on the fact who establishes it. The article's own `competent_authority` still names who decides, which is a different question.

## v0.8.7

An article can say that it requires a fact to exist: a `requires` entry with `fact`, the article's `parameters` the fact holds, and a `description`. A reduction reads the recorded instances of that fact through `source.facts`, named `<regulation>#<article>#<fact>`, which replaces the `source.executograms` of v0.8.6.

Not additive. A reduction that read a stream with `source.executograms` is refused. Declare the fact on the article that requires it, then name it in `source.facts`.

## v0.8.4

An input can call the decision of an article with `source.article`, named as `<regulation>#<article>` or by a `$`-reference to a value that holds one. Without `output` the input is a whole decision value, with the field type `decision`; with `output` it is one output of that decision. `source.parameters_from` merges objects into the parameters of the call, and `derived` on a decision lists outputs computed for it that the decision value leaves out.

Additive: a rulework version valid under v0.8.3 is valid under v0.8.4 unchanged.

## v0.8.2

A new layer, `regulatory_layer: REDUCTIE`. A reduction is a cell's reading of its own chronicle, kept with the cell rather than in the corpus of regulations. It reads the decisions taken under an article with `source.decisions`, which gives a list with one row per decision, until [v0.8.17](#v0817) replaced that with `source.projections`. Only a reduction may read it, and a reduction does not act on law: no `implements`, `hooks`, `overrides`, `execution.produces` or `open_terms`.

Additive: a rulework version valid under v0.8.1 is valid under v0.8.2 unchanged.

## v0.8.1

`execution.produces` may be a list instead of one object, for an article that produces more than one thing, such as an application and the decision on it. Each item has a `type`, a kind of submission or a legal character, and says which of the article's parameters it contains and which outputs it yields. A submission names the article whose decision it asks for with `requests`.

Additive: the object form is unchanged, so a rulework version valid under v0.8.0 is valid under v0.8.1 unchanged.

## Adding a release note

A pull request that adds a schema version adds its note to this page, at the top, under a heading that is the version number alone (`## v0.8.14`). The heading is the anchor the version history links to, so keep anything else out of it. In the same pull request:

1. Link the version cell of the new row in the [version history](/reference/schema#version-history) to the note, as the rows below it do.
2. Say whether a rulework version valid under the previous schema stays valid, and when it does not, what to edit.
3. Take the example from a conformance fixture. Mark the lines to show with `# release-note-begin: <region>` and `# release-note-end: <region>`. Use a fixture under `valid/` for the new construct, or under `invalid/` for a shape the version refuses. Render it with `<FixtureExcerpt fixture="<valid|invalid>/<file>.yaml" region="<region>" />`. If no fixture fits, add one first; [Conformance](/reference/conformance#schema-to-law-model-conformance) and the README next to the fixtures describe how.

`docs/scripts/check-schema-version.mjs` fails CI when a version from v0.8.0 on has no note here, or when its row in the version history does not link to it. A region that is missing or empty fails the site build.
